OCR tutorial: how to OCR an image, the best OCR models, Mistral and DeepSeek OCR

Key takeaways

  • Any image with text can be OCR'd, from phone photos to screenshots; quality of the image decides accuracy.
  • OCR models detect text regions and recognise them; newer vision-language models read whole pages into Markdown or JSON.
  • For printed text, Tesseract and cloud OCR are reliable; for complex layouts and handwriting, AI document models usually win.
  • Multimodal LLMs understand layout and context better but can invent text on unclear areas, so validate outputs.
  • Document images can be classified without OCR using image models or OCR-free document transformers.

Yes, you can OCR almost any image that contains text: phone photos of notes, screenshots, scanned pages and image-only PDFs. OCR, optical character recognition, finds the text and turns it into something you can copy, search or process. This page is a practical tutorial: OCR in a few lines of code, how OCR models work, which models are best including on Hugging Face, whether multimodal LLMs beat OCR, step-by-step use of Mistral OCR, DeepSeek-OCR and NAPS2, and classifying document images without OCR. I built page-batched OCR with Mistral into my OCR & Speech Workspace, so the advice comes from real use.

Can I OCR an image?

Yes. On a phone, Google Lens or Apple's Live Text copy text from any photo. On a computer, OneNote, the Windows Snipping Tool's text actions, Google Lens in the browser, or free tools such as Tesseract extract text from images.

Accuracy depends on the image more than the tool. For best results, use a sharp image with good contrast, straighten tilted pages, crop away backgrounds, and choose the correct language. Screenshots of digital text are the easiest case; handwritten notes, curved book pages and low-light photos are the hardest. For anything important, such as invoices or certificates, check names, dates and numbers after OCR.

What is a simple OCR tutorial?

Install Tesseract, then use Python with pytesseract and Pillow to read an image. This works offline and for free.

Also asked as: tutorial ocr · mistral ocr tutorial · deepseek ocr tutorial · naps2 ocr tutorial

# pip install pytesseract pillow   (and install the Tesseract engine for your OS)
from PIL import Image, ImageOps
import pytesseract

img = Image.open("receipt.jpg")
img = ImageOps.grayscale(img)                         # simple clean-up
text = pytesseract.image_to_string(img, lang="eng")
print(text)

For better results, resize small images up, increase contrast, and try different page segmentation modes with the config="--psm 6" option for uniform blocks of text. To get word positions, use image_to_data. For scanned PDFs, OCRmyPDF adds a searchable text layer in one command.

How does an OCR model work?

An OCR pipeline first detects where text is in the image, then recognises the characters in each region with a neural network, then puts the pieces in reading order. Modern end-to-end models can read a whole page directly.

Also asked as: how ocr model works

Classic deep-learning OCR uses a detection model to find text lines or words, and a recognition model, often a convolutional network with a sequence model and CTC decoding, or a transformer, to read each line. Layout analysis finds columns, tables and reading order. Vision-language models replace the pipeline with one model that sees the page image and generates text, Markdown or JSON, using context from the whole page. That context helps with messy layouts but adds the risk of plausible-looking mistakes.

Which OCR model is the best?

It depends on the document type. For clean printed text, Tesseract and cloud OCR services are accurate and cheap. For complex layouts, tables, forms and handwriting, AI document models from providers such as Mistral, Google and Microsoft, and strong vision-language models, perform best.

Also asked as: best ocr model huggingface

Pick by need:

  • Free, offline, printed text: Tesseract or PaddleOCR.
  • Forms and invoices: Azure AI Document Intelligence or Google Document AI.
  • Whole pages to Markdown with tables: Mistral OCR or a vision-language model.
  • Self-hosted AI OCR: open models such as DeepSeek-OCR on your own GPU.
  • Handwriting: vision-language models, with human review.

Benchmark on twenty of your own documents; public benchmarks rarely match your exact layouts and languages.

What is the best OCR model on Hugging Face?

Hugging Face hosts many OCR and document models: traditional engines such as PaddleOCR, transformer recognisers such as TrOCR, OCR-free document models such as Donut, and newer open document-reading models such as DeepSeek-OCR, plus general vision-language models that read documents well.

Choose based on your task: TrOCR for recognising single lines of printed or handwritten text, Donut for extracting structured fields without a separate OCR step, and modern document vision-language models for full-page conversion to Markdown. Check each model card for licence, supported languages, hardware needs and benchmark results, and try the model's demo space on your own images before integrating it. Newer models appear often, so sort by recent activity and downloads.

Is a multimodal LLM better than an OCR model?

For complex documents, often yes: multimodal LLMs understand layout, tables and context, and can output structured data directly. For simple printed text at scale, dedicated OCR is cheaper, faster and less likely to invent words.

Also asked as: is multimodal llm better than ocr model

The key risk with LLM-based OCR is hallucination on unclear regions: a smudged number may come back as a plausible but wrong value, whereas traditional OCR tends to produce visible garbage. Mitigations include asking the model to mark uncertain text, cross-checking key fields with a traditional OCR pass, validating with rules, such as totals adding up, and keeping page references. A hybrid often works best: OCR for text, an LLM for structure and extraction, then validation, the pattern behind DocuLens AI's schema-validated extraction.

How do I use Mistral OCR?

Mistral OCR is an API that converts PDFs and images into Markdown, page by page, preserving headings, lists and tables, and returning images it finds. You call it with your Mistral API key and a document URL or uploaded file.

# pip install mistralai
from mistralai import Mistral
client = Mistral(api_key="YOUR_KEY")   # read from an environment variable in practice
resp = client.ocr.process(
    model="mistral-ocr-latest",
    document={"type": "document_url", "document_url": "https://example.com/notes.pdf"},
)
for page in resp.pages:
    print(page.index, page.markdown[:200])

Process long PDFs in page batches and store each page's Markdown with its page number, so later answers can cite pages. Check the Mistral documentation for current model names and limits.

How do I use DeepSeek-OCR?

DeepSeek-OCR is an open document OCR model from DeepSeek, available on Hugging Face, that converts document images into text or Markdown. You run it yourself with Python and a GPU, using the Transformers library or a serving engine such as vLLM.

Steps: check the model card for hardware requirements and the exact loading code, install the listed versions of PyTorch and Transformers, load the model and processor with trust_remote_code as the card instructs, then pass page images with the recommended prompt for Markdown output. Because it runs locally, documents stay on your infrastructure, which helps with privacy. Test it against a hosted OCR service on your own pages, checking tables and numbers carefully, before choosing.

How do I use NAPS2 for OCR?

NAPS2, short for Not Another PDF Scanner, is a free, open-source scanning app for Windows, macOS and Linux. Scan or import images, enable OCR with your language, and save a searchable PDF.

Steps:

  • Install NAPS2 from its official website.
  • Scan from your scanner, or import images and PDFs.
  • Open the OCR settings, enable OCR and download the language you need, for example English or Hindi.
  • Reorder, rotate or deskew pages if needed.
  • Save as PDF; the OCR text layer makes it searchable and copyable.

NAPS2 uses Tesseract for OCR, so the same image-quality tips apply: flat pages, good resolution and correct language.

Can you classify document images without OCR?

Yes. You can train an image classifier, such as a fine-tuned vision transformer or a convolutional network, to recognise document types such as invoices, ID cards or certificates from their visual layout, without reading the text. OCR-free document models such as Donut can also classify and extract.

Also asked as: is there any way to classify document image without ocr

Visual classification works well when document types look different, for example a passport page versus a receipt. When types look similar and differ mainly in wording, combining text from OCR with layout features, or using a vision-language model, is more accurate. A practical pipeline classifies first with a fast image model, then runs the right extraction for each type, which saves cost and improves accuracy.
