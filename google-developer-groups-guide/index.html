What is Google Developer Groups? Joining GDG, GDG on Campus and more

Google Developer Groups are local developer communities. How to join, what GDG on Campus is, how organisers are selected, and the Google Developer Program.

Key takeaways

  • Google Developer Groups are independent, volunteer-run local communities for developers interested in Google technologies.
  • Google Developer Student Clubs became Google Developer Groups on Campus in 2024; they are run by student organisers selected each year.
  • Joining a GDG is free: find your chapter on the GDG community site and register for events.
  • Organiser selection rewards a clear plan for the community, evidence that you can run events, and technical credibility.
  • A GDG is worth it if you show up, help and build; the value is the people and the projects, not the badge.

Google Developer Groups (GDG) are local communities of developers interested in Google technologies such as Android, Flutter, Firebase, Google Cloud and Gemini. They are independent, run by volunteer organisers, and host meetups, workshops, study jams and the yearly DevFest conferences. On campuses, the student version, formerly Google Developer Student Clubs, is now called Google Developer Groups on Campus. This page explains each programme, how to join, how campus organisers are selected, how to start a group, whether it is worth it, and how to grow as a developer in Google's ecosystem.

What is Google Developer?

"Google Developer" usually refers to Google for Developers, Google's hub of documentation, tools and programmes for people building with its technologies, or to a developer who builds on Google platforms such as Android, Firebase, Google Cloud or the Gemini API.

Google for Developers includes documentation for every Google API and SDK, codelabs with step-by-step tutorials, and community programmes: Google Developer Groups for local meetups, Google Developer Groups on Campus for students, Women Techmakers, and Google Developer Experts for recognised specialists. There is also the Google Developer Program, a profile with learning badges and benefits. For a student, the useful entry points are codelabs to learn, a local GDG to meet people, and a campus chapter to lead.

What is a Google Developer Group?

A Google Developer Group is a local community of developers who meet to learn and share knowledge about Google technologies. Each chapter is independent, run by volunteer organisers, and supported by Google with resources and event programmes.

Typical activities are talks by local engineers, hands-on workshops, study jams that follow a learning path together, hackathons, and DevFest, an annual community-run developer conference held by chapters around the world. Events are usually free and open to anyone, from students to senior engineers. GDGs exist in many cities across India and worldwide, listed on the GDG community website. They are a good place to meet people working in industry, find teammates for hackathons and practise public speaking in a friendly room.

What is Google Developer Groups on Campus?

Google Developer Groups on Campus are university-based communities for students interested in developer technologies. They were called Google Developer Student Clubs until Google renamed and folded them into the GDG programme in 2024.

Also asked as: how i got selected for google developer groups on campus

Each campus chapter has a student organiser, selected through an application process, and often a core team of volunteers. Chapters run workshops, study jams, solution challenges where students build projects for real problems, and events with GDG and industry speakers. Membership is open to students at the college, regardless of branch or year. For students, it is a structured way to learn technologies like Flutter, Firebase, Google Cloud and Gemini with peers, and to build leadership experience that shows on a CV.

What is Google Developer Student Clubs?

Google Developer Student Clubs, or GDSC, was Google's programme of university clubs for students interested in developer technologies. In 2024 it was renamed Google Developer Groups on Campus and brought under the wider Google Developer Groups programme.

The purpose stayed the same: peer-to-peer learning, hands-on workshops and building solutions for local problems, led by a student organiser. If you see "GDSC" on an older CV, event poster or LinkedIn profile, it refers to the same kind of campus club. The rename connects campus chapters more closely with local city GDGs, which gives students easier access to industry speakers, DevFests and mentors after they graduate.

How do I join a Google Developer Group?

Go to the GDG community website, find the chapter for your city or campus, and join it with your Google account. Then register for an upcoming event; showing up is how membership becomes real.

Also asked as: how to join google developer group

Steps that make it worthwhile:

  • Join your city's GDG and your campus chapter if your college has one.
  • Attend two or three events and talk to the organisers and speakers afterwards.
  • Volunteer: registration desks, social media, logistics. Organisers notice helpers.
  • Offer a short talk or workshop on something you built.
  • Join the chapter's chat group and help answer questions.

There is no fee and no test to join. The people who gain most are the ones who contribute, not the ones who only collect event badges.

How do you get selected as a GDG on Campus organiser?

Students apply when Google opens applications for the new academic year, then go through a review and usually an interview. Selection looks for a clear plan for the community, the ability to organise events, technical curiosity and commitment for the full year.

To strengthen an application:

  • Show you have already built community: a club role, events you ran, a study group you led.
  • Show technical work: projects on GitHub, hackathons, a deployed app.
  • Write a concrete plan: who your members are, three events you will run, how you will reach first-years and non-CS students.
  • Get support from a faculty member.
  • In the interview, be specific and honest about what you have done.

Applications, timelines and eligibility change each year, so watch the official GDG on Campus page.

How do I start a Google Developer Group?

For a city chapter, check that no GDG already covers your area, then apply through Google's GDG programme to become an organiser. For a campus chapter, apply when the GDG on Campus organiser applications open.

Also asked as: how to start a google developer group

Before applying, test the demand: run two or three meetups informally, gather a small core team and keep a list of attendees. Google looks for organisers who will run regular, inclusive, non-commercial events and follow the programme's guidelines. When I founded TechVerse Enclave, now a community of 500+ developers, the lesson was that consistency beats size: a small, regular event where people build things grows faster than an occasional big one. Plan the first quarter's events before you launch, and recruit co-organisers early so the group does not depend on one person.

Is a Google Developer Group worth it?

Yes, if you participate. GDGs give you free learning, access to engineers who work on real products, speaking practice, teammates for hackathons and sometimes job leads. If you only attend occasionally and leave, the value is small.

Also asked as: is google developer group worth it

The benefits compound with involvement: volunteers and speakers get to know organisers, speakers get invited to more events, and organisers build leadership experience and a network that helps for years. For students, a campus chapter is also a low-risk place to practise running projects and teams. The limits are worth knowing: content focuses on Google technologies, event quality varies by chapter, and a GDG badge on a CV means little without projects. Treat it as a community to contribute to, not a certificate.

How do I become a Google developer?

Learn a Google platform deeply and build with it: Android with Kotlin, Flutter for cross-platform apps, Firebase for backends, Google Cloud for infrastructure, or the Gemini API for AI features. Use the official codelabs and documentation.

Also asked as: how to become a google developer

A practical path:

  • Pick one platform that matches the work you want.
  • Complete its official learning path or codelabs.
  • Build and publish two projects, for example an app on the Play Store or a deployed Gemini-powered tool.
  • Earn a relevant certification, such as Associate Android Developer or a Google Cloud certification, if it helps your goals.
  • Share what you learn at your GDG.

Years later, the Google Developer Experts programme recognises people with deep expertise and community contributions; it works by nomination and a review process.

What is the Google Developer Program?

The Google Developer Program is a free membership for developers that gives you a Google Developer profile, learning badges, access to some tools and community features, with an optional paid tier for extra benefits.

Also asked as: what is google developer program

Your developer profile collects badges you earn from codelabs, events and learning pathways, and can be shared like a learning portfolio. It also connects you to community events and developer newsletters. The premium tier has offered extras such as cloud credits and additional learning resources; the exact benefits change, so check the program page. It complements, rather than replaces, real projects: a profile full of badges helps less than one deployed app you can demonstrate and explain.
