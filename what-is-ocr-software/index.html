What is OCR? How OCR software works, the best OCR tools, and OCR vs scanning

OCR turns images of text into editable text. How it works, OCR vs a scanner, whether it is machine learning, the best OCR tools and apps, and Tesseract.

Key takeaways

  • OCR, optical character recognition, converts images of text into machine-readable, editable text.
  • A scanner captures an image; OCR is the software step that reads the text inside it.
  • Modern OCR uses machine learning, from neural text recognisers to vision-language models that read whole pages.
  • For printed documents, built-in tools and Tesseract work well; for messy layouts, tables and handwriting, AI document models do better.
  • Always keep page references and check numbers, names and tables after OCR.

OCR, optical character recognition, is technology that turns images of text, such as scans, photos and image-only PDFs, into editable, searchable text. An OCR scanner is a scanner, or scanner app, that runs OCR after capturing the page. Modern OCR is built on machine learning, and vision-language models now read complex pages, tables and handwriting far better than older engines. I built page-batched PDF OCR into the OCR & Speech Workspace, so this page covers how OCR works, OCR versus scanning, and the best tools, plus a practical Tesseract guide.

What is an OCR scanner?

An OCR scanner is a scanner, scanner app or multifunction printer that captures an image of a document and then runs optical character recognition on it, producing a searchable PDF or editable text instead of just a picture.

Also asked as: what is ocr scanner

Many office scanners and phone scanner apps do this automatically: scan a page and you can select, copy and search its text. The quality depends on the scan, with clean, flat, well-lit pages at a reasonable resolution working best, and on the OCR engine. For printed text, results are near perfect; tables, multi-column layouts, stamps, poor photocopies and handwriting are harder. If text selection works on your scanned PDF, OCR has already been applied.

What does OCR mean on a scanner?

On a scanner, OCR is the option that makes the scanned document's text readable by the computer. With OCR on, the output is usually a searchable PDF, with the image plus an invisible text layer, or an editable document.

Also asked as: what does ocr mean on scanner

When to turn it on: for anything you will search, copy from, translate or feed to software, such as notes, contracts, invoices and textbooks. When it does not matter: photos or documents you only need to view. OCR settings often include the language, which improves accuracy, and output format, such as searchable PDF or Word. Choose the correct language, including Hindi or other Indian scripts where supported, for best results.

What is the difference between OCR and a scanner?

A scanner is hardware or a camera app that captures an image of a page. OCR is software that reads the text in that image. A scan without OCR is just a picture; OCR turns it into text you can edit and search.

Also asked as: difference between ocr and scanner

You can run OCR without a scanner, on a photo or an existing PDF, and you can scan without OCR. Many scanners bundle OCR software, and phone apps combine both steps. For workflows such as digitising notes, processing invoices or building a searchable archive, you want both: good capture for a clean image, then OCR for text. Capture quality often matters more than the OCR engine, so scan straight, flat and well lit.

How does OCR software work?

OCR software cleans up the image, finds where text is on the page, recognises the characters or words in each text region, and assembles the results into lines, paragraphs and layout, often with a confidence score per word.

Also asked as: how ocr software works

The stages:

  • Pre-processing: straighten, remove noise, adjust contrast, binarise.
  • Layout analysis: detect columns, paragraphs, tables and reading order.
  • Text recognition: a neural network reads each line into characters.
  • Post-processing: dictionaries and language models fix likely errors.
  • Output: plain text, searchable PDF, or structured data with coordinates.

Document AI services add field extraction, such as invoice totals and dates, on top of OCR. DocuLens AI does this with schema validation, so extracted fields are checked before use.

Is OCR machine learning?

Modern OCR is machine learning. Today's engines use neural networks, such as convolutional and recurrent networks or transformers, trained on large collections of text images. Early OCR systems used hand-built rules and template matching.

Tesseract, the popular open-source engine, has used an LSTM neural network for text recognition since version 4. Cloud services use deep learning for layout and recognition. Newest of all, vision-language models read a page image directly and output text, tables or structured data, using context much as a person would. Machine learning is why OCR now handles varied fonts, photos and some handwriting, but it also means errors can look plausible, so validation still matters.

What is the best OCR software?

It depends on your documents. For everyday use, the OCR built into Google Lens, Apple's Live Text, Microsoft OneNote and Adobe Acrobat is excellent. For developers, Tesseract is the standard free engine, and cloud services such as Google Cloud Vision, Azure AI Document Intelligence and Mistral's OCR handle complex documents at scale.

Choose by need:

  • Quick text from photos: Google Lens or Live Text.
  • Searchable PDFs from scans: Adobe Acrobat or the free OCRmyPDF.
  • Free and offline: Tesseract.
  • Invoices, forms, tables: Azure AI Document Intelligence or Google Document AI.
  • Complex pages and handwriting: vision-language models and AI OCR services.

Test two tools on ten of your own documents before committing.

What is the best OCR software for PC?

On Windows, Microsoft OneNote and the Snipping Tool's text actions handle quick OCR for free, Adobe Acrobat converts scans into searchable PDFs, and Tesseract or OCRmyPDF give free, offline batch OCR. For complex documents, cloud document AI services do best.

For students and small offices, a practical free setup is: OneNote or the Snipping Tool for copying text from screenshots, and OCRmyPDF to make scanned PDFs searchable in bulk:

ocrmypdf --language eng input.pdf output.pdf

For Hindi or other languages, install the matching Tesseract language data and pass it, for example --language hin+eng. Keep originals, and spot-check output, especially tables.

What is the best OCR scanner app?

On phones, Google Lens and Apple's built-in camera with Live Text are the quickest for copying text, while scanner apps such as Adobe Scan and the scan feature in Google Drive or Apple Notes create multi-page, searchable PDFs.

Look for automatic edge detection, perspective correction, multi-page scanning, OCR language options and export to PDF. For privacy-sensitive documents, prefer apps that process on the device or that you trust with your data. Scanning tips: place pages on a contrasting surface, use even light without shadows, hold the phone parallel to the page, and scan each page separately rather than spreads.

What is the best vision-language model for OCR?

Leading multimodal models from Google, OpenAI, Anthropic and Mistral all read documents well, and specialised OCR models from AI providers are tuned for page-level extraction. The best choice depends on your languages, layouts and cost.

Also asked as: best vision language model for ocr

Vision-language models shine on complex layouts, tables, forms, mixed content and handwriting, and can output structured formats such as Markdown or JSON directly. Their weakness is occasional invented text on unclear regions, which traditional OCR rarely does in the same way. Good practice is to process documents page by page, keep page numbers with every chunk, request structured output, and validate key fields against rules. That is the approach in my OCR & Speech Workspace, where OCR runs in page batches and answers cite pages.

How do I use Tesseract for OCR?

Install Tesseract and its language packs, then run it on an image from the command line or through Python with pytesseract. Clean, high-resolution images give the best results.

Also asked as: a practical guide to ocr with tesseract

tesseract notes.png output -l eng         # writes output.txt

import pytesseract
from PIL import Image
text = pytesseract.image_to_string(Image.open("notes.png"), lang="eng")
print(text)

Improve accuracy by converting to greyscale, increasing resolution, straightening skewed images and choosing the right page segmentation mode with --psm. Use OCRmyPDF to add a text layer to scanned PDFs. Tesseract handles printed text well and struggles with handwriting and complex tables, where AI document models are a better fit.
