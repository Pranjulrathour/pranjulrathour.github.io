Generative AI vs AI, ML, LLMs, predictive AI, agents and OpenAI: the differences explained

How generative AI differs from AI, traditional AI, machine learning, LLMs, predictive, discriminative and explainable AI, AI agents, agentic AI, and OpenAI.

Key takeaways

  • AI is the broad field; generative AI is the part that creates new content such as text, images, code and audio.
  • Traditional and predictive AI classify, score or forecast from data; generative AI produces new outputs.
  • LLMs are one type of generative AI model, specialised in language.
  • Explainable AI is about understanding model decisions, a goal that applies to any AI, including generative models.
  • Agents use generative models to plan and act; OpenAI is a company that builds generative AI, not the field itself.

Artificial intelligence is the broad field of making machines perform tasks that normally need human intelligence; generative AI is the part of AI that creates new content, such as text, images, code, audio and video, rather than only classifying or predicting. ChatGPT, Claude and Gemini are generative AI; spam filters and fraud scores are traditional AI. I build generative AI products such as RAG.NextUpgrad and DocuLens AI, so this page clears up how generative AI differs from AI, ML, LLMs, predictive, discriminative and explainable AI, agents and OpenAI.

What is the difference between generative AI and AI?

AI is the umbrella term covering all techniques that let machines perceive, reason, learn and act, from rule-based systems and search algorithms to machine learning. Generative AI is a subset that generates new content similar to its training data.

Also asked as: difference between generative ai and open ai

Most AI before 2022 was not generative: recommendation engines, image classifiers, speech recognition, route planners and fraud detection. Generative AI became mainstream with large language models and image generators. Both can work together: a document system might use classic AI to classify documents and generative AI to summarise them. Every generative AI system is AI, but most AI systems are not generative.

What is the difference between generative AI and traditional AI?

Traditional AI typically analyses existing data to classify, detect, rank or predict, such as approving loans, recognising faces or forecasting demand, often with task-specific models. Generative AI produces new content and handles open-ended tasks through prompts.

Traditional models are usually smaller, cheaper, more predictable and easier to evaluate with clear metrics like accuracy. Generative models are flexible general-purpose tools but cost more to run, can hallucinate, and are harder to evaluate. Many real systems combine them: traditional models for structured decisions and scoring, generative models for language, summaries and interaction.

What is the difference between generative AI and machine learning?

Machine learning is the approach of learning patterns from data instead of writing explicit rules; it includes classification, regression, clustering and more. Generative AI is built with machine learning, specifically deep learning, but focuses on generating new data.

So generative AI is a subset of machine learning: all modern generative models are trained with ML techniques, but most ML models, such as decision trees predicting churn, are not generative. Learning ML fundamentals, such as data splits, evaluation and overfitting, remains valuable for anyone working with generative AI, because the same principles apply when fine-tuning or evaluating models.

What is the difference between generative AI and an LLM?

An LLM, large language model, is a specific kind of generative AI model trained on huge amounts of text to understand and generate language. Generative AI is broader, including image, video, audio, music and 3D generators as well as LLMs.

Also asked as: difference between generative ai and llm

ChatGPT, Claude and Gemini are built on LLMs, many now multimodal, accepting images and audio. Stable Diffusion and Midjourney are generative image models, not LLMs. LLMs are also used in non-generative ways, such as embeddings for search or classification. In everyday conversation, people often use "generative AI" to mean LLM-based chatbots, but the field is wider.

What is the difference between generative AI and predictive AI?

Predictive AI forecasts outcomes or values from historical data, such as next month's sales, a customer's likelihood to churn or a machine's failure risk. Generative AI creates new content, such as a report, email, image or code.

They answer different questions: predictive AI asks "what will happen?" or "which category?", while generative AI asks "what should this content be?". They combine well: a predictive model flags customers likely to churn, and a generative model drafts personalised retention messages for staff to review. Predictive models are easier to measure with accuracy metrics; generative outputs need human or model-based evaluation.

What is the difference between generative AI and discriminative AI?

In machine learning theory, discriminative models learn the boundary between classes, modelling the probability of a label given the input, such as "is this email spam?". Generative models learn how the data itself is distributed, so they can produce new examples.

Logistic regression, support vector machines and most classifiers are discriminative. Naive Bayes, hidden Markov models, GANs, diffusion models and language models are generative. Discriminative models are often more accurate for pure classification; generative models can create data, handle missing information and model uncertainty. Modern systems frequently combine both approaches.

What is the difference between generative AI and explainable AI?

They address different questions. Generative AI is a type of AI that creates content. Explainable AI is a set of methods and goals for making AI decisions understandable to humans, such as showing which features influenced a prediction.

Explainability matters for all AI, especially in high-stakes areas like lending, healthcare and hiring, where people need to understand and contest decisions. For generative AI, explainability is hard because outputs come from billions of parameters; practical approaches include citations to sources in RAG, showing reasoning steps, and interpretability research into model internals. Grounded answers with citations, as in RAG.NextUpgrad, are a practical form of transparency.

What is the difference between generative AI and AI agents?

Generative AI produces content when prompted. AI agents use generative models, usually LLMs, as their reasoning engine, but add tools, memory and a loop that lets them plan and take actions towards a goal, such as searching, editing files or updating systems.

A chatbot answering a question is generative AI; an assistant that books a meeting by checking calendars, drafting an invite and sending it after your approval is an agent. Agents are built on generative AI but involve more engineering: tool design, permissions, error handling and monitoring. Not every agent is generative, but most modern ones are.

What is the difference between agentic AI and generative AI, with an example?

Generative AI creates outputs in response to prompts; agentic AI pursues goals by deciding and executing multiple steps with tools. Example: asking generative AI "write a leave application" gives you text; asking an agentic system to "apply for leave next Friday" might check your calendar, fill the HR form, submit it after approval and notify your team.

Also asked as: difference between agentic ai and generative ai with example

Agentic systems add autonomy, tool use and planning on top of generative models, which brings more value and more risk. They need guardrails, such as limited permissions, human approval for consequential steps, logs and evaluation of success rates. Many practical systems sit in between: workflows with a few AI-driven decisions.

What is the difference between generative AI and OpenAI?

Generative AI is a field of technology; OpenAI is a company that builds generative AI products, including ChatGPT, GPT models, image and video models, and developer APIs. Many other companies and open-source communities also build generative AI.

Other major developers include Anthropic with Claude, Google with Gemini, Meta with Llama, Mistral, Alibaba with Qwen, and DeepSeek, plus open-source projects worldwide. Saying "OpenAI" when meaning generative AI is like saying a brand name for a whole category. For building products, you can choose among many providers or open models, and multi-provider designs reduce dependence on any one company.
