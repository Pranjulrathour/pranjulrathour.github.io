Hackathons work as time-boxed build events: teams register, receive themes or problem statements, build a prototype in 24 to 48 hours, and demo it to judges for prizes. Thousands run every year, online and in person, with rules on themes, AI tools and age. I have competed in 10+ hackathons, winning first prizes including Changethon 2025 at IIT Roorkee, so this page explains how they work, community views, ChatGPT rules, AI hackathons, timing, frequency, prizes, themes, age limits and where to find them.

How do hackathons usually work?

Registration opens weeks before; teams of two to five form; the event starts with an opening session announcing themes or problem statements; teams build for a fixed period with mentor support; then they submit code and a demo for judging.

Judging usually happens in rounds: a quick screening of all submissions, then finalist presentations to a panel. Criteria commonly include innovation, technical complexity, design, impact and presentation quality. In-person events provide food, Wi-Fi and spaces to work overnight; online events run on Discord or Slack with video demos. Some hackathons are multi-stage, such as idea submissions first and then a build round for shortlisted teams. Read each event's schedule and rules before registering.

What are hackathons, according to the community?

Community discussions describe hackathons as intense, fun weekends of building with friends, learning new tools fast and meeting people, with prizes as a bonus. Veterans stress that the experience and connections matter more than winning.

Common views: beginners learn more in one hackathon than in weeks of tutorials; sponsor-heavy events can feel like marketing, so choose ones with good mentors and fair judging; and polished demos sometimes beat deeper technical work, which frustrates some participants. Many recommend treating hackathons as practice in scoping and shipping. Others warn about burnout from repeated all-nighters, suggesting sleep and realistic goals. Overall, the community sees them as valuable for students building portfolios and networks.

Do hackathons allow ChatGPT?

Most modern hackathons allow ChatGPT and other AI assistants, treating them like any developer tool, but rules vary. Some require disclosure, some ban pre-written code, and a few exam-style or beginner contests restrict AI to keep competition fair.

Organisers increasingly publish explicit AI policies, often allowing assistants for coding help while requiring that the core idea and integration be built during the event, and asking teams to list the tools they used. Judges then focus on originality, product thinking and whether the team understands its code. If the rules are silent, ask organisers before relying heavily on AI. Being open about tools builds trust and avoids disqualification disputes after the results are announced.

How do AI hackathons work?

AI hackathons follow the usual format but centre on building with models: teams use APIs or open models for features like chat assistants, RAG, agents, vision or speech, often with free API credits from sponsors.

Organisers usually provide model access, sample datasets, starter templates and mentors who know the sponsor's tools. Judging emphasises useful applications, responsible design and technical depth, such as evaluation, grounding or clever tool use, rather than a simple wrapper around a chatbot. Strong teams prepare a reusable stack beforehand, such as a FastAPI backend with retrieval and streaming, then spend the event on the problem and demo. Explain how you handle errors and hallucinations, since judges ask.

When do hackathons happen?

Hackathons run all year, but college hackathons cluster around academic calendars, often during the autumn and spring semesters, tech fests and weekends. Large online hackathons and corporate challenges are announced throughout the year.

Also asked as: what are hackathons reddit

Many events run over a weekend, from Friday evening or Saturday morning to Sunday afternoon, so students and professionals can join without missing classes or work. Online hackathons may run for days or weeks with flexible schedules. National events, such as Smart India Hackathon, follow multi-month timelines with idea submissions months before the finale. Follow platforms and communities to see upcoming events, since registration often closes weeks before the hackathon starts.

How many hackathons happen in a year?

Thousands. Platforms such as Devpost, Devfolio, MLH and Unstop together list many thousands of hackathons yearly, from small college events to large global online competitions, plus countless internal company hackathons that are never publicly listed.

MLH alone supports hundreds of student hackathons each season, and Devpost hosts many online challenges at any time. In India, college tech fests, corporate challenges and government events add many more. The exact total is unknown because counting depends on what qualifies, such as ideathons or coding contests. For participants, the takeaway is abundance: you can likely find an event every month that fits your interests and skill level, online or nearby.

Do hackathons have prizes?

Most do. Prizes include cash, gadgets, cloud credits, internships or interview opportunities, incubation support, sponsor products and certificates, with categories such as best overall, best beginner team and best use of a sponsor's API.

Sponsor tracks often offer separate prizes, so a team can win for using a particular tool well even without placing overall. Some events focus on learning and offer only certificates, swag and recognition. Read the prize rules carefully: some require submitting by strict deadlines, joining follow-up programmes, or meeting tax and identity requirements to receive payment. Winning helps, but the project, skills and connections are the lasting rewards, so choose events that match your goals.

Do hackathons have themes?

Many do. Themes or tracks, such as healthcare, education, fintech, sustainability, AI and open innovation, guide what teams build. Some hackathons give specific problem statements, while others are open, letting teams build anything.

Themes help judges compare projects fairly and help sponsors get solutions relevant to their interests. Open innovation tracks reward creativity across domains. Government hackathons such as Smart India Hackathon publish detailed problem statements from ministries and organisations. Choose a theme where you understand the users, have relevant skills or can access data, rather than the most fashionable one. Read theme descriptions carefully, since projects outside the stated tracks may be ineligible for prizes.

Do hackathons have an age limit?

It depends on the event. Many require participants to be at least 18, or allow younger participants with guardian consent; student hackathons require enrolment; and some events have upper limits, such as youth competitions or early-career programmes.

Also asked as: does hackathons have age limit

Student hackathon organisers usually require minors to submit a guardian consent form, and venues or sponsors may add restrictions. School-level hackathons exist specifically for younger students. Corporate and open innovation challenges may require adult participants to sign legal agreements. Always check the official rules and code of conduct, and email organisers if eligibility is unclear, because breaking eligibility rules can lead to disqualification even after winning.

Where can you find hackathons?

Community recommendations include Devpost, MLH's events calendar, Devfolio and Unstop for India, lu.ma and Eventbrite listings, Kaggle competitions, and university tech fest pages. Discord servers and developer communities share upcoming events quickly.

Also asked as: where to find hackathons reddit

Follow organisers and sponsors on LinkedIn and X, join student tech clubs and communities, and subscribe to developer newsletters that list events. Companies such as cloud and AI providers announce hackathons on their blogs. Set alerts on platforms for themes you like. For Indian students, college fest websites and government portals, including Smart India Hackathon, are key sources. Student communities, such as TechVerse Enclave, which I founded, are good places to share opportunities and find teammates.
