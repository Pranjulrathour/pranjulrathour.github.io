How to use Copilot in PowerPoint, why it is missing, and how many Copilots exist

Key takeaways

  • Copilot sits on PowerPoint's Home tab and needs an account with a Copilot plan or licence.
  • Missing Copilot usually means no licence, the wrong account, an old version, privacy settings or admin policy.
  • Copilot drafts a deck in about a minute, but editing for accuracy and story takes much longer.
  • Detailed prompts and well-structured source documents produce better decks.
  • Microsoft's Copilot brand covers dozens of products, with usage limits that vary by plan.

To use Copilot in PowerPoint, open PowerPoint with a Microsoft 365 account that includes Copilot, select Copilot on the Home tab, and type what you want, such as a new deck from a prompt or from a Word document, a summary, or speaker notes. I build AI products and pitch them, so this page covers using Copilot in PowerPoint, finding it, fixing it when it is missing, creating decks, timing, prompts, and how many Copilot products and users exist.

How do you use Copilot in PowerPoint?

Open PowerPoint signed in with an account that has Copilot, select the Copilot button on the Home tab, and type a request in the pane. You can create a presentation, add slides, summarise a deck, organise content or draft speaker notes.

Example prompts: "Create a presentation about our hackathon project for judges, with problem, solution, demo and roadmap slides", or "Summarise this presentation in five bullet points". To build from a document, ask Copilot to create a presentation from a file and reference it, such as a Word document or PDF stored in OneDrive. After Copilot generates slides, edit the text, replace generic images, and check every fact, because the first draft is a starting point, not a finished deck.

Where is Copilot in PowerPoint?

The Copilot button sits on the Home tab of the ribbon, usually at the right end, in PowerPoint for Windows, Mac and the web. Selecting it opens the Copilot pane on the right side of the window.

Also asked as: how to copilot in powerpoint · where to find copilot in powerpoint

In newer versions, Copilot can also appear as an icon on the slide or in a new blank presentation, offering to create a deck. On mobile apps, look for the Copilot icon in the toolbar. If your ribbon is customised or collapsed, the button may be hidden, so reset the ribbon or expand it. If you still cannot see it, your account may not have Copilot access or your app may need an update, as explained in the next answer.

Why is Copilot not showing in PowerPoint?

The usual causes are an account without a Copilot licence or plan, signing in with the wrong account, an outdated version of Microsoft 365, connected experiences turned off in privacy settings, or an administrator disabling Copilot.

Fixes: confirm your plan includes Copilot, and in work accounts ask your admin to assign a licence; sign out and sign in with the licensed account; update Office through File, Account, Update Options; enable connected experiences in File, Options, Trust Center, Privacy Options; and restart PowerPoint. Some features need the file saved in OneDrive or SharePoint. After a new licence is assigned, it can take some time to appear, so restart again later if needed.

Can Copilot create a PowerPoint presentation?

Yes. Copilot can create a full draft presentation from a prompt or from a document, generating a title slide, section slides, bullet text, images and speaker notes, often using your organisation's template if one is set.

Also asked as: can copilot create powerpoint presentation

Quality depends on your input. A detailed prompt with audience, purpose, key points and slide count works better than a single topic word. Creating from a well-structured Word document usually gives the most accurate deck, because Copilot follows the document's headings and content. Expect to rewrite headlines, cut filler, fix image choices and verify facts. Copilot is strongest for first drafts and restructuring, while storytelling, data accuracy and final design still need your judgement.

How long does Copilot take to create a PowerPoint?

Usually under a minute for a draft deck of several slides, depending on the length of your prompt or source document, the number of slides and service load. Long documents and many slides take longer.

The real time cost is editing: plan 15 to 60 minutes to turn a generated draft into a deck you would present, checking facts, tightening wording, adjusting layouts and adding your own data and visuals. If generation stalls, check your connection, try a shorter prompt or smaller document, and retry later, since busy periods can slow responses. For important presentations, generate early, so you have time to rehearse after editing.

How does Copilot assist in PowerPoint presentations?

Copilot drafts new decks, adds slides on a topic, summarises long presentations, answers questions about a deck's content, reorganises slides into sections, rewrites text to be shorter or clearer, generates images, and writes speaker notes.

It is especially useful for turning documents into slides, creating summaries for colleagues who missed a meeting, and preparing notes for rehearsal. It can also help translate or adapt a deck for a different audience. It is weaker at precise data visualisation, complex custom layouts and judgement about what matters most to your audience. Treat it as a fast assistant: you remain the editor responsible for accuracy, story and design consistency.

What is a good Copilot PowerPoint tutorial?

Practise this five-step workflow: write a clear prompt, generate a draft, review the outline, refine with follow-up prompts, then edit manually. Microsoft's support pages include official step-by-step guides with screenshots for each feature.

Also asked as: tutorial copilot powerpoint

A sample session: prompt "Create a 10-slide presentation for first-year students on how to start with AI projects, friendly tone, include a roadmap slide"; then ask "Make slide 3 shorter", "Add a slide comparing Python and JavaScript for beginners", and "Write speaker notes for every slide". Finally, apply your template with Designer, replace stock images with real screenshots, and rehearse with Speaker Coach. Save useful prompts so you can reuse them for future decks.

How many Copilot products are there?

Microsoft uses the Copilot name across dozens of products, so there is no single fixed count. Major ones include Microsoft Copilot, Microsoft 365 Copilot, GitHub Copilot, Copilot Studio, Security Copilot, Copilot in Windows and Edge, and Copilots in Dynamics 365 and Power Platform.

They share branding but differ in purpose, plans and data access: Microsoft Copilot is the consumer assistant; Microsoft 365 Copilot works inside Word, Excel, PowerPoint, Outlook and Teams with your work data; GitHub Copilot helps developers write code; Copilot Studio lets organisations build custom agents; and Security Copilot assists security teams. When reading about Copilot, check which product is meant, since features and availability differ widely between them.

How many Copilot users are there?

Microsoft reported in 2025 that its family of Copilot apps had passed 100 million monthly active users, and GitHub Copilot had passed 20 million all-time users. Figures change quickly, so check Microsoft's latest earnings materials.

These totals combine many products and include both consumer and business use, so they do not tell you how many people use Copilot in PowerPoint specifically. Adoption inside organisations varies: some deploy Microsoft 365 Copilot to all staff, others to small pilot groups. For decisions about your own team, measure usage and time saved in your pilot, since published totals say little about whether the tool fits your workflows.

How many Copilot uses do you get in a day?

It depends on your plan, and limits change over time. Free Microsoft Copilot has usage limits during busy periods, consumer Microsoft 365 plans include a monthly allowance of AI credits for Copilot in Office apps, and business licences follow fair-use limits.

Also asked as: how many copilot uses in a day

If you hit a limit, you will see a message in the Copilot pane; wait for the reset or upgrade your plan. Heavy tasks, such as creating whole presentations or generating images, may use more of your allowance than simple questions. For accurate current limits, check your account page and Microsoft's plan comparison, since Microsoft has revised its consumer and business Copilot plans several times. Plan important work so you are not blocked near deadlines.
