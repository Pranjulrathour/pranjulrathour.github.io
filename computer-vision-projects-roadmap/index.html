Key takeaways

  • Strong projects solve real problems with measurable results and a deployed demo.
  • Ultralytics, Detectron2, Segment Anything and Roboflow are key GitHub resources.
  • Learn from OpenCV basics to detection, transformers and deployment in about six months.
  • CVPR, ICCV and ECCV are the top conferences, and CVIT and IIT groups lead in India.
  • Computer vision understands images, while visual computing also covers graphics and visualisation.

Computer vision teaches machines to understand images and video, from classifying photos to detecting objects, reading documents and recognising faces. The fastest way to learn is building projects with modern pretrained models. I build vision products such as FaceVision, with browser-based detection, recognition and liveness, and the OCR & Speech Workspace for documents, so this page covers project ideas, GitHub repositories, top models, tutorials, a roadmap, conferences, labs in India, Custom Vision and visual computing.

What are good computer vision project ideas?

Good projects solve real problems with measurable results: crop disease detection from leaf photos, a document scanner with OCR, a face recognition attendance system with consent and liveness, traffic or people counting, defect detection for manufacturing, and sign language recognition.

Other ideas include a fitness coach using pose estimation, a retail shelf monitor that spots empty spaces, a waste sorter that classifies recyclables, and a visual question answering tool using a vision-language model. Pick a dataset you can access, define metrics such as accuracy or mean average precision, and deploy a demo. FaceVision is an example of taking a project further: detection, recognition and liveness in the browser, storing embeddings rather than images.

What are the best computer vision projects on GitHub?

Influential repositories include Ultralytics for YOLO detection and segmentation, Detectron2 and MMDetection for research-grade detection, Meta's Segment Anything, Roboflow's supervision and notebooks, OpenCV's samples and Hugging Face Transformers for vision models.

Also asked as: best computer vision projects github

Curated lists such as awesome computer vision collections link papers, datasets and tools. Roboflow's notebooks show end-to-end workflows for training and deploying custom detectors. For learning, study how these projects structure data loading, training and evaluation, then adapt them to your own data. Check licences carefully, since some popular repositories use copyleft licences such as AGPL that affect commercial use. Contributing documentation or examples is a good first open-source step.

What are the best computer vision AI models?

Leading models include YOLO and RT-DETR for real-time detection, Segment Anything 2 for segmentation, DINOv2 for general visual features, CLIP and SigLIP for image-text matching, Depth Anything for depth estimation, and vision-language models such as Qwen2.5-VL and Gemini.

Choose by task and deployment: lightweight YOLO variants run on edge devices, while large vision-language models handle open-ended questions about images but need more compute. Foundation models increasingly allow zero-shot or few-shot use, reducing labelling needs. For specialised tasks, such as medical imaging or document layout, domain-specific models often outperform general ones. Benchmark candidates on your own images and measure speed on your target hardware before committing.

What is a good computer vision tutorial for beginners?

Start with the OpenCV-Python tutorials for image basics, Kaggle Learn's computer vision course for convolutional networks, and fast.ai for practical deep learning. Stanford's CS231n lectures and notes give deeper understanding once you know the basics.

Learn in layers: first load, resize, filter and threshold images with OpenCV; then train an image classifier with transfer learning; then try object detection with a pretrained YOLO model on your own photos. Use Google Colab for free GPU access. PyImageSearch and LearnOpenCV offer many practical walkthroughs. Build a small project after each stage, such as a colour tracker, a classifier for your own photo categories and a custom detector.

What does a PyTorch computer vision tutorial cover?

PyTorch vision tutorials use torchvision for datasets, transforms and pretrained models. Key official tutorials cover training an image classifier, transfer learning with ResNet, and fine-tuning object detection and segmentation models such as Mask R-CNN.

Also asked as: pytorch tutorial computer vision

The TorchVision object detection tutorial fine-tunes a pretrained model on a small pedestrian dataset, teaching custom datasets, bounding box formats and evaluation. Learners then explore the timm library for hundreds of image models and PyTorch Lightning for cleaner training loops. Important skills include GPU usage, data augmentation, mixed precision and exporting to ONNX for deployment. After tutorials, reproduce a simple paper result or train on your own dataset to consolidate understanding.

What is a roadmap to learn computer vision?

Learn Python and NumPy, then image processing with OpenCV, machine learning basics, convolutional networks, detection and segmentation, vision transformers and vision-language models, and finally deployment with ONNX, TensorRT or edge devices.

A six-month plan: month one, Python, NumPy and OpenCV basics; month two, machine learning and image classification; month three, object detection with YOLO; month four, segmentation and pose estimation; month five, transformers, CLIP and vision-language models; month six, deployment, optimisation and a portfolio project. Computer vision engineers also need data skills: labelling, cleaning and evaluating datasets. Mathematics, especially linear algebra and probability, deepens understanding along the way.

What are the best computer vision conferences?

The top computer vision conferences are CVPR, ICCV and ECCV. Major machine learning conferences, such as NeurIPS, ICLR and ICML, also publish important vision research. WACV and BMVC are respected venues, and India hosts ICVGIP.

CVPR runs annually and is the largest, while ICCV and ECCV alternate years. Most papers are freely available through the Computer Vision Foundation's open access site and arXiv. Students can benefit without attending: read award papers, watch recorded talks and follow workshops on topics such as autonomous driving or medical imaging. Volunteering at conferences often provides free registration. Submitting to workshops is a realistic first publication target for student researchers.

What is the best computer vision lab in India?

Strong computer vision research groups in India include CVIT at IIIT Hyderabad, the Vision and AI Lab at IISc, and vision groups at IIT Bombay, IIT Delhi, IIT Madras and IIT Kanpur, which publish at top conferences.

The best lab for you depends on research area, such as document understanding, medical imaging, 3D vision or video, and on faculty whose work interests you. Read recent papers from each group and check their project pages. Students can apply for summer research internships, which many labs offer, or pursue an MTech, MS by research or PhD. Strong applications show projects, programming skill and genuine interest in a lab's specific problems.

What is the difference between computer vision and Custom Vision?

Computer vision is the broad field of enabling machines to understand images and video. Custom Vision is a specific Microsoft Azure service that lets users train image classifiers and object detectors with a simple interface and limited code.

Also asked as: computer vision vs custom vision

Custom Vision suits teams that want to label images, train a model and deploy it without managing machine learning infrastructure, and it can export models for edge devices. Computer vision as a field includes many tools and approaches, from OpenCV to custom PyTorch models and vision-language models. Managed services trade flexibility for convenience. Check Microsoft's documentation for the service's current status and recommended alternatives within Azure AI before starting new projects.

What is the difference between visual computing and computer vision?

Visual computing is the broader field covering all computing with images and visual data, including computer graphics, image processing, visualisation, augmented and virtual reality, and computer vision. Computer vision is the part focused on understanding images.

Also asked as: visual computing vs computer vision

A useful contrast: computer graphics generates images from models, while computer vision infers models from images. The two increasingly merge, as neural rendering, 3D reconstruction and generative image models combine both directions. Universities often group these topics in visual computing departments or courses. For careers, computer vision leads to roles in perception, document AI and robotics, while graphics leads to games, simulation and visual effects, with growing overlap in AI-driven content creation.
