How many jobs has AI replaced or created? Global and India numbers to 2030

How many jobs AI has replaced and created so far, where and how it affects work, WEF projections for 2025 and 2030, and AI jobs and job losses in India.

Key takeaways

  • There is no reliable count of jobs AI has replaced; layoff trackers capture only announced, AI-attributed cuts.
  • AI has hit customer support, content, translation, data entry and some junior coding work hardest so far.
  • AI creates roles in engineering, MLOps, evaluation and governance, plus data centres and chips.
  • The WEF projects 170 million jobs created and 92 million displaced by 2030 across all trends.
  • India has strong AI hiring demand but no official count of AI-caused job losses.

No one has a precise count of jobs replaced by AI, because companies rarely attribute layoffs to a single cause and most AI changes tasks rather than eliminating whole roles. What exists are tracked layoff announcements, surveys and projections, such as the World Economic Forum's estimate of 170 million jobs created and 92 million displaced by 2030. I lead engineering at SCULT INDIA and build AI products, so this page separates measured numbers from forecasts, globally and in India.

How many jobs has AI replaced?

There is no reliable global total. Layoff trackers capture only announcements where companies name AI as the reason, which undercounts quiet changes such as roles not refilled, and overcounts cases where AI is a convenient explanation for cost cutting.

In the United States, Challenger, Gray & Christmas, which tracks announced job cuts, began listing AI as a reason in 2023; AI-attributed cuts have risen but remain a small share of all announced cuts. Larger effects show up indirectly: slower hiring for entry-level writing, support and junior coding roles, and teams doing more work with the same headcount. Treat viral claims of millions already replaced with suspicion unless they cite a clear method and source.

Where has AI replaced jobs?

The clearest effects are in customer support, content writing and translation, data entry and back-office processing, basic graphic design, and some junior programming and testing tasks, where AI tools already handle much of the routine work.

Freelance marketplaces saw early shifts, with fewer simple writing and translation gigs as generative AI spread. Call centres and help desks use chatbots and agents to resolve common queries, reducing demand for first-line staff. Media and marketing teams produce more content with fewer people. In software, AI coding assistants raise productivity, so some companies hire fewer juniors. Physical work, healthcare and skilled trades have seen far less replacement so far, because software alone cannot do those jobs.

How has AI affected jobs?

Mostly by changing tasks within jobs. Workers use AI to draft, summarise, code, analyse and search faster, while expectations rise: employers want people who can use these tools and still check their output.

Effects differ by group. Experienced workers often gain the most because they can judge AI output; some entry-level roles shrink because the routine tasks juniors used to learn on are automated. New skills appear in job descriptions, such as prompt design, AI tool fluency and evaluation. Some industries report productivity gains, others report disappointing pilots. In engineering teams, AI tools speed up routine drafting, while design, review and debugging still need engineers who understand the system.

What jobs has AI created?

AI has created roles such as AI engineer, machine learning engineer, MLOps engineer, data annotator and AI trainer, AI product manager, prompt and conversation designer, AI safety and policy specialist, and AI evaluation and red-teaming roles.

It also expands demand in supporting fields: data engineering, cloud infrastructure, chip design, data centre construction and operations, cybersecurity, and AI governance and compliance. Many existing jobs gained AI components, such as marketers running AI-assisted campaigns and analysts building AI workflows. Some of these roles, like data labelling, are unstable gig work, while engineering and governance roles are in high demand. The pattern matches earlier technologies: new work appears around building, running and regulating the technology.

How many jobs has AI created?

As with losses, there is no precise global count, because AI skills spread across many job titles. Job postings and professional network data show rapid growth in roles requiring AI skills, especially since 2023, though from a small base.

LinkedIn's Economic Graph and similar sources track rising demand for AI engineering and AI literacy across countries, and AI talent hiring has grown faster than overall hiring in many markets. Indirect jobs, in data centres, chips and energy, add more. Counting is hard because a marketer who now uses AI is not a new job, while an AI engineer at a startup is. Watch trends in postings for your target role rather than headline totals.

How many AI jobs were expected to be created by 2025?

The World Economic Forum's Future of Jobs Report 2020 projected that by 2025, about 85 million jobs could be displaced and about 97 million new roles could emerge, across the economies it surveyed, covering automation broadly rather than AI alone.

Now that 2025 has passed, the picture is mixed: generative AI arrived faster than that report expected, but large-scale net job losses did not appear in employment statistics, and many new roles involved data, AI and digital skills. The lesson is that projections describe direction better than exact numbers. The WEF's later reports, in 2023 and 2025, updated the estimates with generative AI included and extended the horizon to 2027 and 2030.

How many AI jobs will be created by 2030?

The WEF's Future of Jobs Report 2025 projects about 170 million jobs created and 92 million displaced between 2025 and 2030 across all trends, a net gain of 78 million, with AI and big data specialists among the fastest-growing roles.

Not all 170 million are AI jobs: the largest absolute growth includes farmworkers, delivery drivers, care workers and sales staff, driven by demographics, the green transition and economic trends. AI-specific roles are smaller in number but grow fastest in percentage terms. The report also expects about 39 percent of workers' core skills to change by 2030, which is why employers emphasise reskilling. Use these projections to choose growing skills, not to predict your personal job security.

How many jobs will AI displace?

Projections vary by method. The WEF expects 92 million jobs displaced by 2030 across all drivers, Goldman Sachs estimated generative AI could expose the equivalent of 300 million full-time jobs to automation, and the IMF found 40 percent of global employment exposed to AI.

Exposure is not displacement: an exposed job may be augmented, with AI taking some tasks and people doing more valuable ones. Displacement depends on adoption speed, regulation, costs and how firms choose to use productivity gains, either to cut staff or to grow output. History suggests technology displaces specific tasks and roles while creating others, but transitions can be painful for individuals. Plan for change in your field rather than betting on any single headline number.

How many AI and ML jobs are there in India?

India has one of the world's largest pools of AI talent and a large number of AI and ML openings, spread across IT services, global capability centres, startups and product companies. Exact counts vary by job portal and date.

Also asked as: how many ai ml jobs in india

NASSCOM and industry reports have repeatedly highlighted strong demand for AI and data skills and a gap between demand and qualified supply, especially for experienced engineers. Job portals typically list thousands of openings for titles such as machine learning engineer, data scientist and AI engineer, with more roles asking for generative AI skills. For an accurate current number, search major portals with consistent filters and track it monthly, since postings change quickly and duplicates inflate counts.

How many jobs have been lost due to AI in India?

There is no official count of jobs lost to AI in India. Companies rarely attribute layoffs to AI alone, and government employment data does not separate AI as a cause, so any precise figure you see is an estimate.

Also asked as: how many jobs lost due to ai in india

What is visible is slower hiring for some entry-level roles in IT services, BPO and customer support, as companies automate routine work and focus on productivity. At the same time, global capability centres and AI-focused startups in India are hiring for new skills. For students and early-career workers, the practical response is to build skills AI cannot easily replace: system design, domain knowledge, communication and the ability to build and supervise AI tools.
