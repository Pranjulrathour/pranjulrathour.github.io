Face recognition FAQ: what it looks for, iPhones, Samsung, iPad, babies, pets, hats

What face recognition looks for, iPhones with Face ID, Samsung setup, iPads, babies, pets, hats, videos and how recognition cameras work.

Key takeaways

  • Face recognition encodes facial structure and texture into an embedding, with depth on Face ID.
  • Face ID is on iPhone X and later, except iPhone SE models, and on iPad Pro from 2018.
  • Samsung registers one face plus an alternative look under Settings, Biometrics.
  • Babies and pets are poor fits for face unlock, though photo apps can group them.
  • Recognition cameras detect, align, embed and match faces, ideally with liveness checks.

Face recognition behaves differently across devices and situations: iPhones use 3D Face ID, Samsung phones use the front camera, and results change with babies, pets, hats or videos. I built FaceVision, which runs face detection, recognition and liveness in the browser and stores embeddings rather than images, so this page covers what face recognition looks for, iPhones with Face ID, Samsung setup and limits, iPads, babies, pets, hats, videos and how face recognition cameras work.

What does face recognition look for?

Face recognition looks at the overall structure of a face: the shape and relative positions of the eyes, nose, mouth, eyebrows and jawline, plus skin texture. Modern systems learn these features automatically and encode them as an embedding.

Systems first detect the face and landmarks, align it to a standard pose, then compute an embedding that captures identity while ignoring lighting and expression as much as possible. Face ID adds a depth map from infrared dots, capturing 3D shape. Accessories, heavy makeup or strong angles can reduce accuracy because they hide features. Good systems focus on stable features, such as the eye region and facial geometry, rather than hairstyles. Understanding this helps explain everyday failures.

Which iPhones have face recognition?

Face ID is available on iPhone X from 2017 and later models, including the iPhone 11 to 16 series and the iPhone 16e. iPhone SE models use Touch ID with a home button instead.

Face ID uses the TrueDepth camera system at the top of the screen, projecting infrared dots to map your face in 3D. iPhone 12 and later support Face ID with a mask, and newer models work in landscape orientation. You can register one appearance plus an alternate appearance. To check your model, open Settings and look for Face ID and Passcode, or find the model in Settings, General, About.

How many faces can you register on Samsung?

Samsung Galaxy phones register one face, with an option to add an alternative look, such as with or without glasses, to improve recognition. You cannot register multiple different people for face unlock.

The alternative look helps when your appearance changes regularly, such as wearing glasses or different hairstyles. Because Samsung's face recognition uses the front camera, it is less secure than fingerprint or PIN, and Samsung recommends stronger methods for sensitive data. To give another person access, they would need their own fingerprint registered or the PIN, which also reduces security. Remove old face data before re-registering if recognition becomes unreliable.

Where is face recognition on Samsung phones?

Open Settings, then Security and privacy, then Biometrics, and tap Face recognition. Enter your PIN, pattern or password, then follow the prompts to register your face. Menu names can vary slightly by model and One UI version.

Also asked as: how many face recognition on samsung

In the same menu, you can add an alternative look, require open eyes, choose faster recognition, and decide whether face unlock stays on the lock screen or opens the home screen directly. To delete your face data, use Remove face data. If you cannot find the menu, use the search bar at the top of Settings and type face. Keep a strong PIN as your backup method.

Can you add face recognition to an iPad?

Not to iPads without Face ID hardware. Face ID requires the TrueDepth camera found only in iPad Pro models from 2018 onward. Other iPads use Touch ID, and no software update or app can add system face unlock.

Also asked as: can i add face recognition to my ipad

Third-party apps can perform face detection using the regular camera for features such as filters, but they cannot replace the system unlock or Apple Pay authentication. If you want Face ID, choose an iPad Pro, and check model details before buying. Otherwise, Touch ID on the top or home button provides secure, convenient unlocking. Keep a strong passcode either way, since it protects the device when biometrics are unavailable.

Does face recognition work on babies?

Not reliably. Babies' and young children's faces change quickly as they grow, so stored templates become outdated, and Apple notes that Face ID may be less reliable for children under thirteen, whose features are still developing.

Photo apps can group pictures of babies, but may split the same child into several groups over time or confuse siblings. Re-confirming groups helps. For device security, parents should not register a baby's face, and children's devices are better protected with passcodes and parental controls. Waiting until children are older avoids most problems. Any system processing children's faces raises extra privacy concerns, and many laws protect children's biometric data more strictly.

Does face recognition work with pets?

Phone face unlock does not work for pets, since it is designed for human faces. However, photo apps such as Apple Photos and Google Photos can recognise and group pets, and some lost-pet services use pet face matching to reunite animals with owners.

Pet recognition uses models trained on animal faces, matching features such as eye placement, nose shape and fur patterns. Services such as Petco Love Lost let owners upload photos of missing pets and compare them with shelter photos. Accuracy is lower than for human faces, especially for breeds with similar appearances, so microchips remain the reliable identification method. Photo grouping works best with clear, front-facing pet photos.

Does face recognition work with hats?

Usually yes. Face ID and most modern systems recognise you wearing hats, caps or scarves, as long as your eyes, nose and mouth remain visible. Hats pulled low over the eyes or hoods casting shadows can cause failures.

Sunglasses can block infrared light on some designs, preventing Face ID from seeing your eyes, and camera-based Android systems are generally less tolerant of accessories. If you often wear a particular hat or glasses, register an alternate appearance with them, and test with your usual accessories during setup. Attendance and access cameras may require removing caps for consistent results, so check guidance where you work or study.

Does face recognition work on videos?

Face recognition can analyse faces in video frame by frame, which security systems and photo apps use to find people in footage. Phone unlock systems, however, reject videos played on screens through liveness and depth checks, at least for 3D systems such as Face ID.

Simple 2D face unlock systems may be fooled by videos, which is why they are not trusted for payments. In video analytics, recognition quality depends on resolution, angle, motion blur and lighting, so it is less accurate than with posed photos. Searching people in video raises serious privacy and legal concerns, requiring clear purpose, authorisation and data protection compliance. FaceVision includes liveness checks for this reason.

How do face recognition cameras work?

A face recognition camera captures video, detects faces in each frame, checks image quality, aligns each face, computes an embedding with a neural network on a built-in chip, and compares it with enrolled embeddings to grant access or log an event.

Many cameras add liveness detection using infrared or depth sensors to resist photos and masks, and anti-tailgating features for doors. Results go to access control panels, attendance software or video management systems. Processing on the device reduces bandwidth and keeps raw video local, while cloud systems centralise analysis. Installation matters: eye-level mounting, consistent lighting and controlled walk paths greatly improve accuracy and speed. Regular calibration and firmware updates keep systems reliable.
