"Mentorship" is spelled m-e-n-t-o-r-s-h-i-p, as one word. Formal mentorships usually last three to twelve months, with meetings of 30 to 60 minutes every two to four weeks, while informal mentoring relationships can continue for years. I have mentored more than 200 students since 2023, so this page covers spelling, how long mentorship and meetings should last, programme length, finding mentorship, mentorship courses, and the nursing and NHS mentorship courses people still search for, including ENB 998.

How do you spell mentorship?

Mentorship is spelled M-E-N-T-O-R-S-H-I-P: the word "mentor" plus the suffix "-ship", which forms nouns describing a state or relationship, as in friendship and leadership. It is one word, without a hyphen or space.

Also asked as: how to spell mentorship

Related words: mentor, mentee, mentoring and mentored. Both "mentorship" and "mentoring" are correct; "mentoring" often describes the activity, and "mentorship" the relationship or programme. Common misspellings include "mentership" and "mentorhip". In formal writing, such as programme names, keep the spelling consistent throughout. The root word comes from Mentor, a character in Homer's Odyssey whose form the goddess Athena took to guide Telemachus, which is why the word has meant a wise, trusted adviser in English for centuries. In job descriptions and resumes, "mentorship" usually appears in phrases such as "mentorship programme" or "provided mentorship to junior developers".

How long is mentorship?

There is no fixed length. Structured programmes commonly last three, six or twelve months; academic programmes often follow a semester or year; and informal mentoring relationships can last many years, evolving into friendships or professional partnerships.

The right length depends on the goal: preparing for placements might take a few months, while growing into a leadership role can take years. Many relationships have intense phases around key decisions and lighter phases otherwise. Some of the most valuable mentors are people you contact a few times a year at important moments. A senior who helped you choose electives in first year can be just as useful again when you weigh internship offers in third year.

How long should a mentorship last?

A mentorship should last as long as it serves clear goals and both people find it valuable. For structured programmes, six months is a common sweet spot: long enough to make progress, short enough to stay focused.

Also asked as: how long should mentorship last · how long should a mentorship program last

Set a review point, such as after three months, to check progress and decide whether to continue, change focus or close. Ending a formal mentorship is healthy, not a failure: reflect on what you achieved, thank your mentor, and keep in touch. Relationships that end with clear achievements often continue informally because both sides value the connection.

How long should a mentorship meeting be?

Most mentorship meetings work well at 30 to 60 minutes. Shorter check-ins of 15 to 20 minutes suit quick updates; longer sessions suit reviewing work, such as a portfolio or a talk rehearsal.

Make meetings effective with structure: the mentee shares updates and one or two specific questions in advance, the conversation focuses on those, and both agree on next actions before ending. Respect the agreed time, especially with busy mentors. Regularity matters more than length: a consistent 30-minute meeting every two weeks beats occasional two-hour sessions.

How long should a mentorship programme last?

Formal programmes typically last three to twelve months, with six months common in workplaces and an academic year common in colleges. Onboarding buddy programmes may last only the first few months of a job.

Design the length around goals: short programmes for specific outcomes, such as interview preparation, and longer ones for development, such as leadership. Include a kickoff, midpoint review and closing reflection. Programmes that are too long without milestones lose momentum, while those too short end before trust forms. Offer participants the option to continue informally afterwards.

How do I find mentorship?

Look in formal programmes at your college, workplace or professional association, and build informal relationships through communities, alumni networks, events, hackathons, LinkedIn and free mentorship platforms. Approach people with specific questions and show your work.

Also asked as: how to find mentorship

Practical steps: identify what help you need; find people two to five years ahead on that path; engage with their work; send a short, specific message asking for a brief conversation; prepare questions; and follow up with what you did. Communities such as student tech clubs create natural mentoring, which is how TechVerse Enclave's seniors came to guide juniors.

Is there a mentorship course?

Yes. Mentorship training courses teach mentors and mentees how to build effective relationships: setting goals, active listening, giving feedback, coaching questions, boundaries and evaluating progress. They are offered by universities, professional bodies, companies and online platforms.

Also asked as: course mentorship · mentorship course for nurses

Many organisations train mentors before programmes start, often in short workshops. Professional fields such as healthcare and teaching have formal mentoring or supervision training linked to their standards. Online courses provide general frameworks, but practice with feedback is where skills grow. Even a one-hour training on asking good questions and giving useful feedback improves mentoring quality noticeably. Mentees benefit from training too: learning to set goals, prepare questions and act on feedback makes every session more productive.

What mentorship courses are there for nurses?

Nurse mentorship and preceptorship programmes prepare experienced nurses to support students and newly qualified nurses in clinical practice: teaching skills, assessing competence, giving feedback and supporting wellbeing. Requirements depend on each country's nursing regulator.

In the United Kingdom, the Nursing and Midwifery Council's standards for student supervision and assessment define roles such as practice supervisor and practice assessor, with preparation provided by universities and employers. Other countries have their own frameworks, such as clinical instructor or preceptor programmes. Nurses should check their regulator's current standards and their employer's training offers.

What is the NHS mentorship course?

In the NHS, mentorship training for nurses and midwives historically meant regulator-approved mentor preparation courses. Since the NMC introduced new standards for student supervision and assessment, NHS trusts and universities provide preparation for practice supervisors and practice assessors instead.

Also asked as: mentorship course nhs

Many trusts also run preceptorship programmes for newly qualified staff and leadership mentoring schemes. Courses are usually short, delivered with partner universities, and include e-learning plus workshops. Check your trust's education team or the partner university for current offerings, since structures vary between regions. Completing supervision and assessment preparation is also useful for career progression into education or leadership roles in nursing.

What was the ENB 998 mentorship course?

ENB 998, from the former English National Board for Nursing, Midwifery and Health Visiting, was a well-known course in teaching and assessing in clinical practice. It prepared nurses to mentor and assess students on placements.

Also asked as: mentorship course enb 998

The English National Board was replaced in 2002, and mentor preparation later followed Nursing and Midwifery Council standards. Since the move to the NMC's standards for student supervision and assessment, the old mentor qualifications have been replaced by practice supervisor and assessor preparation. Nurses who completed ENB 998 or equivalent courses may still list them, but current roles require preparation under today's standards.
