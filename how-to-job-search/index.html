How do I job search? A practical guide with LinkedIn, Google and AI tools

Job searching works best as a weekly system: target roles, search the right platforms, tailor each application, track replies. Using LinkedIn, Google and AI.

Key takeaways

  • Treat the job search as a weekly system: a target list, a fixed number of tailored applications, and a tracker.
  • Set saved searches with alerts on LinkedIn, Indeed and Google's job results so new roles come to you within a day.
  • Use AI to tailor and check your resume and to practise interviews, not to mass-apply with bots.
  • Referrals and direct messages to engineers convert far better than cold applications through a portal.
  • Job search fatigue is normal; protect your energy with a routine, small targets and time off from the portals.

To job search well, decide on two or three target roles, set saved searches with alerts on LinkedIn, Indeed, Naukri and Google's job results, tailor your resume for each application, ask for referrals, and track every application in one sheet. Treat it as a weekly system, not a daily scroll. This page shows how to search on each platform, what a job search engine is, how to use AI tools without hurting your chances, which strategies work for students and freshers, and how to handle job search fatigue.

How do I job search?

Pick a specific target, such as "junior Python backend developer" or "GenAI intern", then run a weekly routine: find roles, tailor and send a set number of applications, follow up, and practise interviews.

Also asked as: what is job search strategies

A routine that works for students and early-career engineers:

  • Target list: 20 to 30 companies you would genuinely join, plus two or three role titles.
  • Alerts: saved searches on LinkedIn, Indeed, Naukri, Internshala and Google so new roles reach you within a day.
  • Applications: five to ten tailored ones a week beat fifty generic ones.
  • Referrals: message engineers at target companies with a short note and your best project.
  • Tracker: company, role, date, contact, status and next step in one spreadsheet.

Review the tracker every Sunday and adjust what is not getting replies.

How do I job search on LinkedIn?

Open the Jobs tab, search a precise title, then filter by date posted, experience level, location and remote options. Save the search with alerts on, and apply to fresh roles within a day or two.

Make your profile searchable first: a headline with your target role and core skills, a featured section with your best project, and skills that match the job descriptions you want. Use Boolean search in the keyword box, for example "machine learning" AND (intern OR fresher) NOT senior. Easy Apply is quick but crowded, so for roles you care about, also find someone on the team and send a short message. Recruiters notice candidates who post about what they build, so share project updates regularly.

How do I job search on Google?

Search a role plus "jobs", for example "data analyst jobs India" or "remote Python developer jobs". Google shows a jobs panel that gathers listings from many sites, with filters for date, type and location.

Also asked as: how to job search on google

Open the panel to filter by date posted, job type and employer, and turn on alerts for the search so new listings arrive by email. Each listing links to where you can apply, often the company's own careers page, which is usually the best place to apply. Google can show these listings because employers mark up job posts with structured data. Search operators help too: site:greenhouse.io "machine learning intern" or site:lever.co "backend engineer" surfaces roles posted on the applicant tracking systems many startups use.

How do I job search on Indeed?

Enter a job title or skill and a location, or "remote", then filter by date posted, job type, experience and salary details where shown. Upload a resume, create a profile and set job alerts for your main searches.

Also asked as: how to job search on indeed

Indeed aggregates listings from company sites as well as roles posted directly on it, so it is wide but noisy. Sort by date and focus on listings from the last few days. Read the full description before applying and match your resume's wording to the skills it asks for, because many employers screen applications with keyword filters. Be careful with listings that ask for payment, personal documents early, or chats on messaging apps before any interview; genuine employers do not charge candidates.

What is a job search engine?

A job search engine collects job listings from many sources, such as company career pages, job boards and recruiters, and lets you search them all in one place. Indeed and Google's job results are examples.

Also asked as: what is job search engine

A job board, by contrast, mainly hosts listings that employers post directly on it. In practice the line blurs: LinkedIn, Naukri and Indeed both host and aggregate. For freshers in India, combine one or two general engines with specialist sources: Internshala for internships, Wellfound for startups, the careers pages of companies you admire, and community job channels in developer groups. The engine finds roles; your application still has to stand out.

Which AI job search tool is best?

There is no single best one. The most useful AI help is a general assistant such as ChatGPT, Claude or Gemini used to tailor your resume, prepare for interviews and research companies, plus the AI features already built into LinkedIn and job platforms.

Also asked as: which ai job search is best

Use AI for:

  • Comparing your resume with a job description and listing the gaps.
  • Rewriting bullet points to show impact, then checking every claim is true.
  • Drafting short referral and follow-up messages in your own voice.
  • Mock interviews: ask for technical and behavioural questions for the exact role.

Be wary of auto-apply tools that send hundreds of applications for you. They often produce poor matches, can break platform rules and can leave a bad impression with recruiters who see the same generic message twice.

Does AI job search work?

AI makes the preparation faster and better; it does not get you hired on its own. Tailored applications, strong projects and referrals still decide outcomes, and AI helps you produce those faster.

Where it clearly helps is speed: tailoring a resume to a job description takes minutes instead of an hour, and mock interviews are available at any time. Where it hurts is when output is generic. Recruiters read many AI-written cover letters that sound the same, and a resume stuffed with keywords but no evidence fails the first human read. The test is simple: could you defend every line in an interview? If not, rewrite it. Your projects, your GitHub and the way you talk about what you built carry more weight than any tool.

How do I use AI for job search?

Give the assistant the job description and your resume, ask for the three biggest gaps and a tailored version, then edit it yourself. Use it the same way for messages, company research and interview practice.

Also asked as: how to use ai job search

A prompt that works: "Here is a job description and my resume. List the skills the role needs that my resume does not show. Then rewrite my project bullets to match the role, using only facts from my resume." Paste the result into your own document and fix the voice. For interviews: "Ask me five interview questions for this role, one at a time, and grade my answers." Never paste personal documents such as ID numbers into an assistant, and never let it invent experience or numbers.

What job search strategies actually work?

The strategies that work are targeting, referrals, proof of skill and consistency: a focused list of roles, warm introductions, public projects that match those roles, and a steady weekly pace.

For students and freshers in tech, ranked roughly by return on effort:

  • Referrals: a short message to an engineer at the company, with a link to a relevant project.
  • Projects: one or two deployed projects that match the role, with a clear README.
  • Hackathons and communities: they create contacts and stories for interviews.
  • Tailored applications through company career pages.
  • Cold applications on large portals, kept as a background routine.

When I speak to first-years, the roadmap I give is foundations first, then hackathons and internships, then placements or a startup. The job search is easier when the earlier steps are done.

What is job search fatigue?

Job search fatigue is the exhaustion and loss of motivation that builds up after weeks of applications, rejections and silence. It is common, and it usually shows as procrastination, low-effort applications or giving up on good roles.

Ways to manage it:

  • Set process goals you control, such as five tailored applications a week, instead of outcome goals.
  • Time-box the search to fixed hours and keep other parts of life, such as learning, exercise and friends, running.
  • Keep building one project during the search so you make visible progress.
  • Track replies to see what works, and change your approach rather than only increasing volume.
  • Talk to peers or a mentor; feedback on your resume often fixes the real problem fast.

If low mood persists for weeks, talk to someone you trust or a professional.
