Who are web developers? The job, who hires them, demand, remote work and how to start

Who web developers are, what they do, who hires them, how many are self-taught or remote, demand, courses, becoming one step by step, and freelancing.

Key takeaways

  • Web developers build and maintain websites and web apps: front end, back end or full stack.
  • They are hired by tech companies, agencies, startups, enterprises, governments and freelance clients.
  • Many developers learned partly or fully through online resources; degrees help but are not required everywhere.
  • Remote and hybrid work are common in web development, more so than in most professions.
  • Become one by learning HTML, CSS, JavaScript and a back end, building real projects, and showing them.

Web developers are the people who build and maintain websites and web applications: front-end developers create what users see and interact with, back-end developers build servers, databases and APIs, and full-stack developers do both. They work at tech companies, agencies, startups and enterprises, or as freelancers. I build web products with Next.js, FastAPI and PostgreSQL and lead engineering at SCULT INDIA, so this page covers who web developers are, their work, who hires them, self-taught and remote developers, demand, courses, becoming one and freelancing.

Who are web developers?

Web developers are programmers who specialise in the web: they write HTML, CSS and JavaScript for interfaces, and languages such as JavaScript, Python, PHP, Java or Go for servers, working with databases, APIs and cloud hosting to make websites and web apps work.

Also asked as: who hire web developers

They come from varied backgrounds: computer science graduates, BCA and MCA students, bootcamp graduates and self-taught programmers. Many specialise, for example in front-end frameworks like React, back-end APIs, e-commerce, performance or accessibility. Increasingly, web developers also integrate AI features, such as chat assistants and document processing, into web applications. Their work powers everything from college websites and online shops to banking portals and SaaS dashboards.

What does web developer work involve?

Daily work includes building features from designs and requirements, writing and reviewing code, fixing bugs, connecting front ends to APIs, designing databases, testing, deploying, improving performance and security, and collaborating with designers, product managers and other developers.

Also asked as: what is web developer work

A typical feature flows from planning to implementation, code review, testing and release. Developers also maintain existing code, update dependencies and respond to incidents. Communication matters as much as coding: understanding requirements, explaining trade-offs and documenting work. Tools include code editors, Git, browser developer tools, package managers, CI pipelines and, increasingly, AI coding assistants.

What is the web developer job like?

The job combines problem solving, building and continuous learning. Roles include front-end, back-end, full-stack, WordPress or e-commerce developers, and titles range from junior and associate to senior, lead and architect.

Also asked as: what is web developer job

Work settings vary: product companies focus on one product deeply; agencies build many client projects quickly; startups need generalists who do everything; and enterprises have larger teams and processes. Day to day, expect a mix of focused coding, meetings, code reviews and debugging. Technologies change fast, so ongoing learning is part of the job. Many developers find the work rewarding because they see their creations used by real people. Pay and growth depend heavily on skills, the type of employer and the quality of your portfolio.

Who hires web developers?

Web developers are hired by software product companies, IT services and consulting firms, digital and marketing agencies, startups, e-commerce businesses, banks and fintechs, media companies, educational institutions, governments, and freelance clients such as small businesses.

In India, large IT services companies hire many freshers, while product companies and startups often hire through skills tests and portfolios. Agencies value speed and versatility; startups value ownership. Freelance platforms and direct client relationships provide independent work. Networking, hackathons and open source often lead to opportunities that never appear on job boards. Referrals from people who have seen your work remain one of the strongest ways in.

How many web developers are self-taught?

Developer surveys such as Stack Overflow's consistently find that most developers learned at least partly through online resources like documentation, videos and tutorials, and a significant share report being largely self-taught, even many who also hold degrees.

Self-teaching works in web development because resources are abundant and free, and portfolios can prove skill. Degrees still help with some employers, structured fundamentals and campus placements. The best approach combines both: formal education where available, plus self-directed projects that show you can build real things. Employers increasingly assess skills directly through projects and coding tests. That shift rewards anyone willing to build consistently, regardless of where they studied.

How many web developers work remotely?

Remote and hybrid work are common in web development, more than in most professions, since the work needs only a computer and internet. Developer surveys show a large share of developers working fully remote or hybrid, though many companies have shifted towards office or hybrid policies.

Remote roles are competitive, so they often favour experienced developers with strong communication and proven independent work. Freelancers usually work remotely by default. To be remote-ready, practise written communication, documentation and self-management, and build a public portfolio. For freshers, in-person or hybrid roles often provide faster learning through mentorship. Many developers start in office roles and move to remote work once they have experience and references.

How in demand are web developers?

Web developers remain in steady demand because nearly every organisation needs websites, web apps and internal tools. Competition is strongest at entry level, while experienced developers with modern frameworks, cloud and AI integration skills are especially sought after.

AI tools are changing the work, automating routine coding and increasing productivity, but they raise the value of developers who understand architecture, security, performance and user needs. Developers who can build AI-powered features, such as RAG assistants or document processing in web apps, have an advantage. Specialising while keeping broad fundamentals is a strong strategy. Accessibility, performance and security skills are also increasingly valued as regulations and user expectations rise.

What is a web developer course?

A web developer course teaches the skills to build websites and web apps: HTML, CSS, JavaScript, a front-end framework, a back-end language, databases, version control and deployment. Options include free curricula, paid bootcamps, university courses and certifications.

Also asked as: what is web developer course

Strong free choices are MDN's learning area, freeCodeCamp and The Odin Project, which emphasise building projects. Choose courses with real projects, current technologies and community support. Avoid courses that promise jobs without serious practice. Whatever course you take, build your own projects alongside it, because portfolios get interviews.

How do I become a web developer step by step?

Learn the fundamentals, build projects of increasing complexity, learn a back end and databases, deploy everything, build a portfolio, and apply for internships and jobs while contributing to communities.

Also asked as: how to be a web developer step by step

Steps:

  • HTML and CSS: build static pages and responsive layouts.
  • JavaScript: interactivity, the DOM, fetching APIs.
  • A framework such as React or Next.js.
  • A back end with Node.js or Python and FastAPI, plus SQL databases.
  • Git, testing and deployment to Vercel or a cloud.
  • Three portfolio projects solving real problems.
  • Internships, hackathons and open-source contributions.

How do I become a freelance web developer?

Build a portfolio of real projects, define a niche and services, set up a professional profile, find first clients through your network, local businesses or freelance platforms, and deliver excellent work that earns referrals and reviews.

Also asked as: how to web developer freelance

Tips: start with small, well-defined projects; use written agreements covering scope, timelines, payments and ownership; communicate clearly and often; handle hosting, domains and maintenance as optional services; and keep learning. Specialising, for example in e-commerce or business websites with booking systems, helps you stand out. Manage taxes and invoicing properly from the start.
