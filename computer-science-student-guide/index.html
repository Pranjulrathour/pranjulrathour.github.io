What do computer science students learn? Studying CS, jobs, internships and laptops

What CS students learn and how to study it, duration, why students drop out, IES eligibility, jobs, internships, whether a MacBook suits, and why choose CS.

Key takeaways

  • CS students learn programming, data structures, algorithms, systems, networks, databases, maths and electives such as AI.
  • Study by building: pair every course topic with a small project and practise problems regularly.
  • Degrees usually take three or four years in India; self-study fundamentals takes one to two years of steady work.
  • Computer Science is not one of the UPSC ESE disciplines, so CS graduates generally cannot take the IES exam.
  • Internships come from projects, open source, hackathons, platforms and alumni, not just campus placements.

Computer science students learn how computers and software work and how to build them: programming, data structures, algorithms, computer architecture, operating systems, networks, databases, software engineering, discrete mathematics, and electives such as AI and security. I am a BCA student at VSICS and have mentored over 200 students since 2023, so this page covers what CS students learn and how to study and start, course length, why students drop out, IES eligibility, jobs, internships, laptops, and reasons to choose CS.

What do computer science students learn?

Computer science students learn programming and the theory behind it: data structures, algorithms, discrete maths, computer organisation, operating systems, networks, databases, compilers and software engineering, plus electives in AI, machine learning, security, graphics or web development.

Early years focus on programming fundamentals, maths and logic; later years on systems and specialisations, with projects and often an internship. Indian programmes, B.Tech CSE, BCA and B.Sc CS, differ in depth: B.Tech is more engineering-heavy, BCA more application-focused. Across all of them, the skills that matter most for jobs are solid programming, problem solving, and the ability to build and ship working software.

How should I study computer science?

Study actively: write code for every concept, build small projects, solve problems regularly, and explain what you learn to others. Reading notes before exams is not enough for a subject that is learned by doing.

Also asked as: how to study computer science

A weekly rhythm that works:

  • Attend or watch lectures, then implement the concept the same week.
  • Solve a few data structures and algorithms problems several times a week.
  • Build one project per semester connected to your interests.
  • Use version control from day one and push work to GitHub.
  • Join a club, community or study group.
  • Teach juniors; explaining exposes gaps.

Students I mentor who build alongside coursework consistently do better in interviews than those who only study for exams.

How do I start learning computer science?

Start with one programming language, usually Python or C, learn fundamentals through a structured course such as Harvard's free CS50, then move to data structures and algorithms while building small projects you care about.

Also asked as: how to start learning computer science

A beginner roadmap:

  • Months 1 to 2: programming basics, problem solving, Git.
  • Months 3 to 4: data structures and algorithms fundamentals.
  • Months 5 to 6: a domain, such as web development or data, with two projects.
  • Then: databases, operating systems and networks basics.
  • Throughout: build in public, join a community and attend hackathons.

Free resources from MIT OpenCourseWare and the "Teach Yourself Computer Science" guide cover the deeper theory when you are ready.

How many years does it take to study computer science?

In India, a B.Tech or B.E. in Computer Science takes four years, a BCA or B.Sc in Computer Science usually three years, and an MCA or M.Tech two years. Many new four-year undergraduate programmes follow recent education policy changes.

Also asked as: how many years to study computer science

Self-learners can cover core fundamentals in one to two years of consistent study, and become employable sooner in a focused area, such as web development, with strong projects. Learning never really ends in this field, because tools change every few years. What matters to employers is demonstrated skill, so use whichever path you choose to build a portfolio, not only to collect a degree.

Why do computer science students drop out?

Common reasons include difficulty with maths and programming early on, choosing CS under family or peer pressure without real interest, falling behind and losing confidence, financial pressures, mental health struggles, and feeling that coursework is disconnected from real jobs.

Prevention helps: ask for help early from faculty, seniors or mentors; join study groups; focus on fundamentals rather than comparing yourself with peers who started coding earlier; and connect learning to projects you care about. If CS is truly not right, switching is not failure; many adjacent fields, such as design, product management and data analysis, value similar skills. Colleges with active mentoring and peer communities tend to keep more students engaged.

Can a computer science student take the IES exam?

Generally no. The UPSC Engineering Services Examination, often called IES, covers four disciplines: Civil, Mechanical, Electrical, and Electronics and Telecommunication Engineering. Computer Science is not one of them, so CS graduates usually are not eligible.

Also asked as: can computer science student give ies exam

Check the latest UPSC notification for exact eligibility, since rules can change. Alternatives for CS students interested in government roles include GATE-based recruitment to public sector undertakings, scientist and engineer recruitment at organisations such as ISRO and DRDO, the Civil Services Examination, banking and SSC technical posts, and state government IT positions. Each has its own syllabus, so prepare specifically.

What jobs can computer science students get?

CS graduates work as software developers, web and mobile developers, data analysts and scientists, machine learning and AI engineers, DevOps and cloud engineers, cybersecurity analysts, QA engineers, product managers, technical writers and researchers, among many others.

Also asked as: what are jobs for computer science students

Entry routes include campus placements, off-campus applications, internships converting to offers, startups, and freelancing. AI engineering is growing fast: building RAG systems, fine-tuning models and deploying AI features. Whatever role you target, employers look for problem solving, communication, and proof of building things. A portfolio of two or three real projects, deployed and documented, often matters more than grades alone for startups.

How do computer science students find internships?

Build two or three real projects, keep a clean GitHub and resume, then apply widely through platforms such as Internshala, LinkedIn and company career pages, reach out to alumni, contribute to open source, and use hackathons to meet companies.

Also asked as: how to find internships for computer science students

Tips:

  • Apply early; many internships open months before they start.
  • Tailor your resume to each role's skills.
  • Message startups directly with a short note and a project link.
  • Apply to programmes for students, such as those run by large tech companies.
  • Participate in hackathons, where sponsors often recruit.
  • Ask professors about research internships.

Hackathons worked for my network: several students I know found internships through people they met at events.

Is a MacBook good for computer science students?

Yes, a MacBook is good for most CS work: it is fast, has long battery life, and macOS is Unix-based, which suits development. But it is not required, and some tasks are harder on it, such as certain Windows-only tools and NVIDIA GPU work.

Also asked as: is macbook good for computer science students

Consider your needs: for web, mobile and general programming, a MacBook works well, and it is required for iOS development. For machine learning with CUDA, a Windows or Linux laptop with an NVIDIA GPU, or cloud GPUs, is better. For tight budgets, a Windows laptop with at least 16 GB of RAM and an SSD, running WSL or Linux, does almost everything CS courses require. Check your college's software requirements before buying.

Why choose computer science?

Choose computer science if you enjoy solving problems, building things and learning continuously. It offers wide career options, the ability to create products that reach many people, flexible work, and skills that apply across every industry.

It is also demanding: technology changes constantly, competition for entry-level jobs is strong, and coursework requires steady effort. Choose it because you are curious about how software and systems work, not only because of trends. If you are unsure, try a free course such as CS50 and build a small project; enjoying that process is a better signal than any ranking or salary survey.
