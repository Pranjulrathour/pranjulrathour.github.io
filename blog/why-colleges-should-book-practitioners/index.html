Why colleges should book practitioners, not only professors, for AI sessions

The case for bringing working engineers onto campus: currency of the toolchain, the language of production, honest career information, and a network students cannot get from a syllabus.

Professors give students the foundations that outlast every framework. Practitioners give them the present tense. The colleges that produce employable AI graduates book both, and I have watched the difference from the stage at VSICS and from the judging tables of student hackathons.

What a practitioner brings that a syllabus cannot

  • Currency. The retrieval, fine-tuning and deployment tooling changes every semester. Someone who shipped last month knows which parts changed.
  • The language of production. Confidence gates, evaluation sets, structured logs, fallbacks — the vocabulary interviews are conducted in.
  • Honest career information. What a GenAI role actually involves day to day, and what recruiters actually read (see portfolio over certificates).
  • A network. Practitioners hire, refer and invite. A good session is the start of internships and hackathon teams.

What professors bring that practitioners cannot

Depth, continuity and assessment. A visiting speaker cannot teach linear algebra in an afternoon or grade a semester. The right model is co-teaching, described in faculty and practitioners co-teaching GenAI — faculty own the semester, practitioners own the build sessions and reviews.

Choosing the right practitioner

  • Someone who has shipped recently and can show it running, not only describe it.
  • Someone close enough in age and background that students believe the path is open to them. A practitioner from a similar college does more for a tier-2 batch than a distant celebrity.
  • Someone who will do hands-on work and written feedback, not only a keynote.

What it costs

Often nothing. Many practitioners speak free for student communities and ask only for travel. What it requires is a coordinator who sends a proper invitation — the checklist is in how to invite a GenAI guest speaker — and a department willing to give a working engineer the room for two hours.

Book the practitioner for currency and the professor for depth, and let them meet in the same classroom. The students in that room are the ones who get hired.
