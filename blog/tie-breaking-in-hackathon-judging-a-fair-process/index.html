Tie-breaking in hackathon judging: a process that doesn't feel arbitrary to the teams

A coin flip or an organiser's gut call at the top of the leaderboard erodes trust fast. A tie-breaking process decided before the event, not during it.

A tie at the top of the leaderboard, resolved by an organiser's gut call in the final ten minutes, is the single fastest way to make a hackathon's results feel arbitrary to every team that didn't win.

Decide the process before the event, not during it

  1. First tiebreaker: the highest-weighted rubric criterion alone (execution, in most rubrics) — whoever scored higher there wins the tie.
  2. Second tiebreaker: a live follow-up question to both teams, scored blind by the panel without discussion.
  3. If still tied, both teams share the placement rather than a coin flip deciding it.

Tell teams the process upfront

Publishing the tiebreak rule alongside the rubric, before the event starts, means nobody feels like the rule was invented to produce a particular winner. See a hackathon judging rubric organisers can copy.
