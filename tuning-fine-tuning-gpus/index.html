What does tuning mean in machine learning? Fine-tuning, GPUs and GPU tuning

Tuning vs fine-tuning vs hyperparameter tuning, whether fine-tuning retrains the whole model, GPUs and clouds for it, GPU tuning, and vLLM on AMD.

Key takeaways

  • Tuning means adjusting something for better performance; in ML it usually means hyperparameter tuning or fine-tuning a model.
  • Fine-tuning does not have to retrain the whole model: LoRA and QLoRA train small adapters while the base model stays frozen.
  • For LLM fine-tuning, GPU memory matters most; QLoRA lets 7B to 8B models fit on a single 16 to 24 GB GPU.
  • GPU tuning means adjusting clocks, power and fans; undervolting can cut heat and noise for long training runs.
  • vLLM supports AMD GPUs through ROCm, alongside NVIDIA GPUs.

Tuning means adjusting something to make it work better. In machine learning it has two common meanings: hyperparameter tuning, choosing settings such as learning rate before or during training, and fine-tuning, continuing to train a pretrained model on your own data. "GPU tuning" is different again: adjusting a graphics card's clocks, power and fans. This page explains each meaning, whether fine-tuning retrains the whole model, which GPUs and clouds suit LLM fine-tuning, whether and how to tune an NVIDIA GPU, and vLLM on AMD, drawing on building FineTune Studio, a browser-based QLoRA fine-tuning tool.

What is the meaning of tuning?

Tuning means making small adjustments to improve performance or fit a purpose, like tuning a guitar, an engine or a radio. In computing, it means adjusting settings of software or hardware to run faster, more accurately or more efficiently.

In AI, you will meet several kinds: hyperparameter tuning, fine-tuning a model, prompt tuning, performance tuning of databases and inference servers, and GPU tuning. They share the idea of adjusting a system against a measurable goal. The key habit in all of them is measurement: change one thing, measure the effect, keep what helps.

What is the meaning of fine-tuning?

Fine-tuning means taking a model that has already been trained on a large general dataset and training it further on a smaller, specific dataset, so it adapts to a particular task, domain, format or style.

Also asked as: what is the meaning of fine tuning

For example, a general language model can be fine-tuned on support tickets to classify them into your categories, or on examples of your reports to follow your format. Fine-tuning is much cheaper than training from scratch because the model already knows language; you only teach the specifics. It changes behaviour well, but is weaker for adding facts, where retrieval-augmented generation is usually better.

What is tuning in machine learning?

In machine learning, tuning usually means hyperparameter tuning: finding the best values for settings that are not learned from data, such as learning rate, batch size, number of trees, regularisation strength or model depth, by testing combinations on validation data.

Methods include grid search, which tries every combination; random search, which samples combinations and is often more efficient; and Bayesian optimisation, which uses past results to choose promising settings. Always tune on a validation set and report final results on a separate test set, or you overfit to your evaluation. Libraries such as scikit-learn and Optuna make this straightforward. For LLM fine-tuning, the important hyperparameters are learning rate, number of epochs, LoRA rank and batch size.

Does fine-tuning require retraining the entire model?

No. Full fine-tuning updates all weights, but parameter-efficient methods such as LoRA freeze the original model and train only small added matrices, often well under one per cent of the parameters. QLoRA also loads the frozen model in 4-bit precision to save memory.

The benefits: far less GPU memory, faster training, tiny adapter files that can be swapped on one base model, and less risk of damaging the model's general abilities. Quality is close to full fine-tuning for many tasks. Full fine-tuning still makes sense for large changes in behaviour or domain when you have lots of data and compute. FineTune Studio uses QLoRA through Transformers, PEFT and TRL so fine-tuning fits on modest GPUs.

What is the best GPU for fine-tuning LLMs?

The most important factor is GPU memory. With QLoRA, 7B to 8B models fit on GPUs with 16 to 24 GB; larger models need 40 to 80 GB data-centre GPUs or several GPUs. NVIDIA GPUs have the broadest software support.

Also asked as: best gpu for fine tuning llm

Rough guidance:

  • Learning and small models: free cloud GPUs in Google Colab or Kaggle notebooks.
  • 7B to 8B models with QLoRA: consumer GPUs with 24 GB, or cloud GPUs such as the L4.
  • 13B to 34B models: 40 to 80 GB GPUs such as A100 or H100 class, or multi-GPU setups.
  • Very large models: multiple data-centre GPUs and distributed training.

Memory also depends on sequence length and batch size; long contexts need much more. Measure peak memory on a short run before a long one.

Big vs small GPU clouds for fine-tuning LLMs: which is better?

Large cloud providers offer reliability, enterprise security, many regions and integration with other services, at higher prices and with quota hurdles for GPUs. Smaller GPU clouds often offer cheaper, easier access to GPUs by the hour, with fewer extra services.

Also asked as: big vs. small gpu clouds for fine-tuning llms

For students and individual experiments, smaller GPU clouds and notebook platforms are usually the most practical: quick to start, pay only for training time. For companies with compliance needs or data already in a major cloud, the big providers simplify security and data transfer. Whichever you use, save checkpoints to persistent storage, shut instances down when finished, set budget alerts, and keep training scripts reproducible so you can move between providers.

What is GPU tuning?

GPU tuning means adjusting a graphics card's operating settings, such as core and memory clock speeds, voltage, power limit and fan curve, to get more performance, lower temperatures, less noise or better efficiency.

There are two directions. Overclocking raises clocks for more performance, at the cost of heat, power and possible instability. Undervolting or power limiting lowers voltage or power for nearly the same performance with less heat and noise, which is often ideal for long machine learning runs. Tools include vendor utilities and third-party apps. Changes should be small and tested for stability, because an unstable GPU can crash a training run after hours.

Should I tune my GPU?

For machine learning, modest power limiting or undervolting is often worth it: it reduces temperature, noise and power draw, usually with little loss of speed, which helps on long training runs. Aggressive overclocking is rarely worth the instability risk for training.

Before tuning, make sure drivers are up to date, cooling and airflow are good, and dust is cleared, since these often matter more. Change one setting at a time and run a stress test or a short training job to check stability. On laptops, manufacturer power modes are safer than manual tuning. If you are not comfortable, leave defaults; modern GPUs already manage boost clocks and thermals automatically.

How do I tune an NVIDIA GPU?

Use NVIDIA's own app or a third-party tool to adjust power limit, clocks and fan curve, or nvidia-smi on the command line to set a power limit, then test stability with a real workload.

Also asked as: how to tune nvidia gpu

A cautious approach for machine learning:

  • Check current power, temperature and clocks with nvidia-smi.
  • Lower the power limit step by step, for example in small percentages, and measure training speed.
  • Keep the setting where speed barely drops but temperature and noise fall clearly.
  • Adjust the fan curve if temperatures stay high.
  • Run a full training epoch to confirm stability.

Setting power limits may need administrator rights and resets after reboot unless made persistent.

Does vLLM support AMD GPUs?

Yes. vLLM supports AMD GPUs through AMD's ROCm software platform, in addition to NVIDIA GPUs with CUDA, and it also has support for other accelerators. Check the documentation for supported AMD models, ROCm versions and installation steps.

On AMD, installation typically uses ROCm-specific Docker images or builds, and some features may arrive later than on NVIDIA. Before committing, verify that your exact GPU and ROCm version are supported, and benchmark throughput with your model and settings. vLLM's strengths, efficient memory management and continuous batching, apply on both platforms, which makes it a good choice for serving open models to many users.
