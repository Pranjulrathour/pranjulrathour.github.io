How to get your first tech job, and what FIRST Tech Challenge is

How to land, keep and grow in your first tech job: CVs, why it feels hard, how long to stay, recovering from a firing. Plus what FIRST Tech Challenge is.

Key takeaways

  • Your first tech job comes from proof, not promises: two or three deployed projects, an internship or freelance work, and referrals.
  • A first-job CV is one page, led by projects with links and results, with skills that match the job description.
  • It feels hard because entry-level roles attract huge numbers of applicants and AI tools have raised the bar; specific, visible work cuts through.

  • FIRST Tech Challenge is a school robotics competition where teams design, build and program robots for a new game each season.

"First tech" usually means one of three things: your first job in the tech industry, FIRST Tech Challenge, a school robotics competition, or First Tech Federal Credit Union, a US bank. This page is about the first two. Most of it covers how to get your first tech job, write the CV, why it feels so hard, how to survive the first months, how long to stay and how to recover if it goes wrong. The last two sections explain FIRST Tech Challenge for students interested in robotics.

What is First Tech?

"First Tech" most often refers to someone's first job in tech, to FIRST Tech Challenge, a robotics competition for school students run by the non-profit FIRST, or to First Tech Federal Credit Union, a credit union in the United States.

If you came here about banking, First Tech Federal Credit Union's own website and support line are the right places for opening hours and account details. This page focuses on careers and robotics. For students and freshers in India, the career question is the one that matters most: how to get from a degree to a first role as a developer, data analyst, AI engineer or similar, and how to do well once you are there. FIRST Tech Challenge is relevant if you are in school and want hands-on engineering experience.

How do I get my first tech job?

Build proof of skill, make it visible, and apply in a targeted way: two or three deployed projects, an internship or freelance work, an active GitHub and LinkedIn, and referrals to the companies you want.

Also asked as: how to get first tech job

A plan that works:

  • Pick a lane: backend, frontend, data, mobile or AI. Depth beats a long skills list.
  • Build projects that solve a real problem, deploy them, and write clear READMEs.
  • Get experience: internships, freelance work for local businesses, open source or hackathons.
  • Network: message engineers at target companies with a short note and your best project.
  • Apply consistently: five to ten tailored applications a week, tracked in a sheet.
  • Practise interviews: coding problems and explaining your projects aloud.

Expect it to take months, not weeks.

How do I write a CV for my first tech job?

Keep it to one page, put projects near the top with links and results, list skills grouped by type, and tailor the wording to each job description. Recruiters skim, so every line must earn its place.

Also asked as: how to write a cv for your first tech job

Structure for a fresher:

  • Header: name, email, phone, GitHub, LinkedIn, portfolio.
  • Projects: name, problem, stack, what you built, a measurable result, live link.
  • Experience: internships and freelance work, with outcomes.
  • Skills: languages, frameworks, databases, tools, cloud.
  • Education: degree, college, years, CGPA if strong.
  • Achievements: hackathons, competitive programming, relevant certifications.

Avoid skill bars, photos and objective statements. Use the same words as the job description where they are true, because many companies screen CVs automatically before a person reads them.

Why is landing your first tech job harder than expected?

Because entry-level roles attract very large numbers of applicants, many companies prefer people with some experience, and AI tools have raised the bar for juniors. Generic CVs and certificate lists no longer stand out.

Also asked as: why landing your first tech job is way harder than you expected

There is also a mismatch between what college teaches and what jobs need: deploying, debugging, working with Git, writing tests and explaining trade-offs. Many applicants apply through the same portals at the same time, so cold applications have low reply rates. What changes the odds is specificity: a project that matches the role, a referral, a hackathon result, or a short message that shows you understand the company's product. Rejections are normal and rarely personal; track them, adjust one thing at a time, and keep building.

How can Gen Z land their first tech jobs?

Gen Z candidates have an advantage in using AI tools and building in public; use both. Ship projects with AI help that you fully understand, document them publicly, and use communities and hackathons to meet people who hire.

Also asked as: how generation z can land its first tech jobs

What works for this generation of graduates:

  • Post short build logs on LinkedIn or X while you work on a project.
  • Join developer communities and contribute: answer questions, run a session, help organise an event.
  • Enter hackathons; they compress learning, teamwork and networking into a weekend.
  • Learn to use AI coding tools well, and be ready to explain every line they wrote.
  • Look beyond big brands: startups and small companies often hire on proof of skill.

How do I survive my first tech job?

Focus on learning the codebase, asking good questions, delivering small things reliably, and communicating clearly. Nobody expects a fresher to know everything; they expect you to learn fast and be dependable.

Also asked as: how to survive your first tech job

Habits that help in the first months:

  • Write down how to set up, build, test and deploy the project as you learn it.
  • Ask questions after trying for a reasonable time, and show what you tried.
  • Take small tickets first and finish them properly, with tests.
  • Read code reviews carefully and apply feedback the next time.
  • Give short, regular updates so your lead never has to chase you.
  • Keep a "brag document" of what you shipped, for appraisals and your next move.

Protect your health; late nights every week are a warning sign, not a badge.

How long should I stay at my first tech job?

Long enough to finish meaningful projects and learn deeply, which usually means around eighteen months to two years. Leave earlier if the job is harming your health, stopped your learning completely, or you have a clearly better opportunity.

Also asked as: how long to stay at first tech job

Staying a reasonable time lets you see projects through, from building to running in production, which is where much of the learning is. It also gives you stories for your next interviews. Leaving very quickly is fine once; a pattern of short stints raises questions. Before switching, make sure you are moving towards something: a bigger scope, a better team or a field you care about, not only away from a bad week.

How do you recover after getting fired from your first tech job?

Take a short pause, understand honestly what went wrong, fix the gap, and start applying again with a calm, factual explanation. Being let go early in a career is more common than people admit, and it is recoverable.

Also asked as: how i recovered after getting fired from my first job in tech

Steps that help:

  • Ask your former manager or HR for specific feedback if you can.
  • Separate causes you control, such as skills or communication, from ones you do not, such as layoffs or team changes.
  • Spend a few weeks closing the main skill gap and ship a small project that shows it.
  • Prepare a short, honest answer: what happened, what you learned, what you do differently now.
  • Lean on mentors and peers for referrals.

Keep your finances and health steady during the search; panic decisions rarely help.

What is FIRST Tech Challenge?

FIRST Tech Challenge is an international robotics competition for students roughly aged 12 to 18, run by the non-profit FIRST. Teams design, build and program robots to play a new game released at the start of each season.

Teams usually have up to fifteen students with adult mentors. They learn mechanical design, electronics, programming, project management and outreach, and they keep an engineering portfolio of their work. Awards recognise not only match results but also design, innovation, outreach and teamwork. For a school student interested in engineering or computer science, it is one of the best hands-on experiences available, and it gives material for college applications and later interviews. Check FIRST's website for teams and events near you.

How does FIRST Tech Challenge work?

Each season FIRST announces a game. Teams then build a robot from a kit of reusable parts, program it in Java or a block-based tool, and compete in matches where alliances of teams score points in autonomous and driver-controlled periods.

A match starts with a short autonomous period in which the robot runs code with no human control, followed by a driver-controlled period. Teams form alliances, so collaboration matters as much as competition. Events progress from local qualifiers to regional or national championships and an international championship. Judging also looks at the team's engineering portfolio and interviews. The programming side is real software engineering: sensors, control loops, computer vision for detecting game elements, and testing under time pressure.
