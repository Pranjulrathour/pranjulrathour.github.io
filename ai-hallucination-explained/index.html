What is AI hallucination? Why LLMs hallucinate and how to prevent it

AI hallucination is when a model states something false as fact. Why LLMs hallucinate, how to test for it, and how to reduce it in ChatGPT and RAG systems.

Key takeaways

  • An AI hallucination is a fluent, confident answer that is false or unsupported by the sources.
  • LLMs hallucinate because they generate likely text, not verified facts, and training often rewards guessing over saying 'I don't know'.
  • Grounding answers in retrieved documents with citations, plus a refusal path on weak evidence, cuts hallucinations the most.
  • Test with questions that have known answers and questions the sources cannot answer, and measure faithfulness.
  • Hallucinations are shrinking with better models but will not reach zero; design systems that can be checked.

In everyday language, a hallucination is perceiving something that is not there. In AI, a hallucination is when a model produces a confident, fluent answer that is false or not supported by its sources, such as an invented citation, a wrong date or a made-up API function. This page explains AI hallucinations in models and ChatGPT, why they happen and what research says, whether they can be prevented, how to test for them, and the techniques that work best, including in enterprise RAG systems, drawing on how I built RAG.NextUpgrad to refuse rather than guess.

What does hallucination mean?

Hallucination generally means a perception without a real stimulus, such as seeing or hearing something that is not there; it is a medical and psychological term. In AI, the word was borrowed to describe models producing content that sounds right but is not true or not grounded.

Also asked as: what is hallucination meaning

The AI meaning is a metaphor: models do not perceive anything, they generate text. Some researchers prefer "confabulation" because the model fills gaps with plausible inventions. Whatever the name, the behaviour is the same: the output reads confidently but is wrong or unsupported. If you are asking about hallucinations as a health symptom, speak to a doctor; this page is about AI.

What are hallucinations in AI models?

AI hallucinations are outputs that are fabricated or unsupported: wrong facts, invented quotes and citations, non-existent people or products, incorrect code, or claims that contradict the provided documents.

Two kinds are worth separating. Factual hallucinations contradict real-world facts, such as a wrong founding year. Faithfulness hallucinations contradict or go beyond the given context, such as a summary that adds a claim not in the document. In applications built on your documents, faithfulness is what you measure and control. Hallucinations also occur in image models, for example extra fingers or garbled text, and in speech recognition, where words appear during silence.

What are hallucinations in ChatGPT?

In ChatGPT, hallucinations are answers that state false information confidently: made-up references, wrong numbers, invented features of software, inaccurate summaries of a document, or fabricated details about a person.

They are more likely with niche topics, recent events, exact figures, long calculations, and questions that assume something false. Ways to reduce them: turn on search for current facts and open the sources; upload the document and ask for answers only from it, with quotes; ask the model to say when it is unsure; use the code tool for calculations; and verify anything important. The well-known case of lawyers citing fake cases generated by a chatbot shows why checking sources is essential.

Why do LLMs hallucinate?

LLMs generate the most probable next words based on patterns in training data; they do not look up verified facts. When knowledge is missing, rare or ambiguous, the most plausible-sounding continuation can be wrong, and the model states it as fluently as a correct one.

Also asked as: why llm hallucinate · why llm hallucinate paper

Contributing causes:

  • Training objective: predicting likely text rewards plausibility, not truth.
  • Rewarding guesses: training and evaluation often score a confident guess better than "I don't know".
  • Knowledge gaps and cutoffs: rare or recent facts are poorly represented.
  • Long or noisy context: important details get lost, especially in the middle of long inputs.
  • Pressure from the prompt: questions with false premises or demands for specifics push the model to invent.

Retrieval, citations and permission to abstain address several of these at once.

Which papers explain why LLMs hallucinate?

A good starting point is the 2022 survey of hallucination in natural language generation by Ji and colleagues, which defines types, causes and mitigation. OpenAI also published research in 2025 arguing that standard training and evaluation reward guessing over admitting uncertainty, which encourages hallucination.

Related reading: the original retrieval-augmented generation paper, which showed that grounding a model in retrieved passages improves factual answers; work on long contexts showing that models use information at the start and end better than the middle; and RAG evaluation work such as RAGAS for measuring faithfulness. Reading these together gives a clear picture: hallucination comes from how models are trained and used, and the fixes are grounding, calibration and evaluation.

Can AI hallucinations be prevented?

They cannot be eliminated completely, but they can be reduced a lot. Grounding answers in trusted sources, requiring citations, letting the model say "I don't know", checking outputs automatically and keeping people in the loop for high-stakes decisions all help.

Effective techniques:

  • Retrieval-augmented generation with good retrieval and reranking.
  • Instructions to answer only from provided context and to quote it.
  • A confidence gate: refuse when retrieval scores or evidence are weak.
  • Structured outputs validated against schemas and rules.
  • Tools for facts and maths, such as search and code execution.
  • Human review for medical, legal, financial and safety-critical uses.

The aim is a system where errors are rare and detectable, not a model that is never wrong.

How do I test an LLM for hallucination?

Build a test set with questions that have known answers in your documents, plus questions your documents cannot answer, then measure how often answers are faithful to the sources and how often the model correctly declines.

Also asked as: how to test llm hallucination

A practical method:

  • Collect 50 to 100 real questions with reference answers and source passages.
  • Add 10 to 20 unanswerable or false-premise questions.
  • Score faithfulness: is every claim supported by the retrieved context?
  • Score abstention: does it decline the unanswerable ones?
  • Use an LLM judge for scale and check a sample by hand.

Track these numbers every time you change prompts, models or retrieval. Frameworks such as RAGAS automate faithfulness and context metrics.

How do I make AI use uploaded documents without hallucinating?

Make the documents the only allowed source: retrieve the relevant passages, instruct the model to answer only from them with quotes or page citations, and to say clearly when the answer is not in the documents. Then check citations automatically.

Also asked as: how to make ai reliably use uploaded documentation without hallucinating

Practical steps:

  • Split documents into sensible chunks that keep headings and page numbers.
  • Retrieve with hybrid keyword and vector search, then rerank.
  • Put only the top passages in the prompt, labelled with IDs.
  • Require a citation for each claim and reject answers whose citations do not match.
  • Allow and encourage "not found in the documents".

This is how the OCR & Speech Workspace answers document questions with page citations, and how RAG.NextUpgrad refuses when evidence is weak.

What is the most effective way to reduce hallucinations in enterprise RAG systems?

Improve retrieval first, then enforce grounding. Most enterprise RAG hallucinations happen because the right passage was not retrieved, so hybrid search, reranking and good chunking deliver the biggest gains, followed by citation checks and a refusal threshold.

Also asked as: what’s the most effective way to reduce hallucinations in enterprise rag systems

A proven stack:

  • Clean sources: remove outdated and duplicate documents; add metadata.
  • Hybrid retrieval: BM25 plus vectors, merged with reciprocal-rank fusion.
  • Reranking: a cross-encoder on the top candidates.
  • Grounded generation: answer only from cited passages.
  • Confidence gate: decline when evidence scores are low.
  • Evaluation and monitoring: faithfulness tests on every change, and logs of user questions.

RAG.NextUpgrad combines these and runs in about 220 MB on a free tier, so this is achievable without heavy infrastructure.

Will AI hallucinations go away?

They are decreasing as models improve and as training rewards admitting uncertainty, but they are unlikely to disappear entirely, because models generate probable text and the world contains questions they cannot know the answer to.

The practical future is systems that hallucinate rarely and make errors easy to catch: grounded in sources, showing citations, calibrated about confidence, and checked by tools and people where it matters. For users, the habit that lasts is verification: open the source, test the code, and treat confident answers about important matters as claims to check. For builders, design for detectability from the start.
