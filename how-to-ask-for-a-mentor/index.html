How to ask someone to be your mentor, questions to ask, mentor vs teacher and origins

Key takeaways

  • Ask for specific, small help first, act on the advice, and let mentorship grow from follow-ups.
  • Bring concrete context and specific questions to every career mentor conversation.
  • Teachers instruct groups within a curriculum; mentors guide an individual's broader growth.
  • Mentor comes from Homer's Odyssey, and the modern sense spread through Fénelon's 1699 novel.
  • Great mentors combine high standards, real responsibility and genuine care.

To ask someone to be your mentor, start with a specific, small request, such as one conversation about a decision, show you have done your homework, and let the relationship grow from follow-ups rather than a formal ask. I have mentored more than 200 students since 2023, so this page covers how to ask, what to ask a career mentor, how mentors differ from teachers, the Greek origin of the word, where mentorship came from, mentor duties, mentoring founders and academics, and what makes a great mentor.

How do you ask someone to be your mentor?

Do not open with "will you be my mentor". Ask for something specific and small: fifteen minutes of advice on a decision, feedback on a project, or how they handled a situation you face. Show what you have already tried.

Also asked as: how to ask a mentor to be a mentor · ask hn: who has been your top mentor

Example message: I am a second-year BCA student building a RAG project and saw your talk on retrieval; could I ask you two questions about evaluating it, over a short call or by message? Afterwards, act on their advice and report back with results. If conversations continue naturally, you can propose a regular rhythm, such as monthly check-ins. Respect their time, accept a no gracefully, and remember that mentorship grows from demonstrated effort.

What questions should you ask your career mentor?

Ask questions that draw on their experience and your specific situation: what skills mattered most early in their career, what they would do in your position, which mistakes to avoid, and how they evaluate opportunities.

Also asked as: what questions should i ask my career mentor

Useful questions: What would you focus on in my first two years? How do you decide between two offers? What separates good engineers from great ones in your team? How did you get your first leadership responsibility? What should I stop doing? Which people or communities should I connect with? Bring context each time, such as a project, a decision or a draft resume, so answers are concrete rather than generic.

Is a mentor a teacher?

A mentor teaches, but is not a teacher in the formal sense. Teachers deliver structured lessons to groups and assess learning; mentors guide an individual's broader growth through experience, advice and example, usually outside a curriculum.

Also asked as: is mentor a teacher

A teacher can become a mentor when the relationship extends beyond the classroom, such as advising a student on career choices or projects. Likewise, a senior engineer mentoring a junior teaches skills, but without exams or a syllabus. The word mentor emphasises a personal, ongoing relationship and the mentee's goals, while teacher emphasises instruction and knowledge transfer. Both roles matter, and the best teachers often act as mentors for some students.

What is the difference between a mentor and a teacher?

Teachers follow a curriculum, teach groups, set assignments and assess performance within a course. Mentors focus on one person's goals, offer guidance from experience, and support decisions about careers, skills and personal growth over time.

Also asked as: difference between mentor and teacher

Other differences: teaching is usually formal and time-bound, while mentoring is often voluntary and open-ended; teachers have authority over grades, while mentors usually have no authority over the mentee; and teaching transfers defined knowledge, while mentoring develops judgement and confidence. Mentors can be peers, seniors or professionals outside your field. Students benefit from both: teachers build foundations, and mentors help apply them to real choices and opportunities beyond the syllabus.

Is mentor a Greek word?

Yes. Mentor comes from Greek mythology: in Homer's Odyssey, Mentor is an old friend of Odysseus who is entrusted with the care of his son, Telemachus. The goddess Athena takes Mentor's form to guide Telemachus.

The modern sense of mentor as a wise adviser became popular through the 1699 French novel The Adventures of Telemachus by François Fénelon, in which Mentor plays a central guiding role. From there, the word entered English and other languages as a common noun. The verb to mentor appeared much later, in the twentieth century. The story's lesson still fits today: a mentor guides growth through presence, wisdom and encouragement.

Where did mentorship first come into practice?

Mentorship is ancient: experienced people have guided younger ones in families, crafts and philosophy for millennia, such as Socrates teaching Plato. The idea took its name from Homer's Odyssey, and craft apprenticeships formalised it for centuries.

Medieval guilds paired apprentices with masters for years of practical training, combining skill transfer with guidance on conduct and career. Formal workplace mentoring programmes spread in the 1970s and 1980s, and Kathy Kram's 1985 book Mentoring at Work shaped how organisations understand career and psychosocial mentoring. Today mentoring appears in schools, universities, companies, startups and online communities, often supported by structured programmes and platforms that match mentors and mentees.

What is a mentor's job description?

A mentor's role is to guide a mentee's development: meet regularly, help set goals, share experience, give honest feedback, suggest resources and connections, encourage reflection, and maintain confidentiality and professional boundaries.

Also asked as: what is mentor job description

In formal programmes, a mentor description often lists the time commitment, such as one hour every two weeks for six months; required experience; responsibilities, such as preparing for sessions and tracking goals; and expectations around safeguarding, especially with students or minors. Paid mentor roles on education platforms add teaching sessions, reviewing work and reporting progress. Good mentors listen more than they talk, ask questions before advising, and help mentees make their own decisions rather than deciding for them.

Why is mentorship essential for entrepreneurs?

Entrepreneurs face many first-time decisions, about customers, hiring, fundraising and product, with little margin for error. Mentors who have built companies help them avoid costly mistakes, see blind spots, make introductions and stay resilient through setbacks.

Also asked as: why mentorship is essential for entrepreneurs

Accelerators and incubators build programmes around mentorship for this reason. Useful mentors challenge assumptions, share specific experiences rather than generic advice, and connect founders to customers, investors and talent. Founders should seek several mentors with different strengths, such as product, sales and finance, and test advice against their own data, since every startup's context differs. Experienced outside perspectives often reveal problems that a team is too close to see.

How do you mentor someone in academia?

Academic mentoring helps students and early researchers with research skills, writing, publishing, conferences, funding and career choices. Set expectations early, meet regularly, give specific feedback on work, and support independence rather than doing work for them.

Also asked as: how to mentor anyone in academia

Good academic mentors explain unwritten rules, such as how peer review works or how to approach potential collaborators, introduce mentees to networks, and discuss careers both inside and outside academia. They model research integrity and help mentees handle rejection, which is common. Agree on authorship and responsibilities early to avoid conflict. For students from first-generation or underrepresented backgrounds, mentors can make a large difference by making hidden expectations visible.

What makes someone a top mentor, according to developers?

When developers describe their best mentors in community discussions, common themes appear: the mentor gave real responsibility early, explained the reasoning behind decisions, reviewed code patiently and honestly, and believed in them before they believed in themselves.

Many recall a senior engineer who let them make manageable mistakes, then helped them learn from them, or a manager who shielded them from noise while pushing them to grow. Others mention authors and open-source maintainers whose writing or reviews acted as mentorship at a distance. The pattern is clear: great mentors combine high standards with genuine care. If you mentor others, aim for both, and your mentees may name you one day.
