Key takeaways

  • A pitch deck is a short persuasive presentation, while business plans and memoranda give depth.
  • Proposals answer a client's or funder's requirements, while decks pitch a venture.
  • Portfolios show past work, treatments pitch stories, and company profiles introduce established firms.
  • Use landscape 16:9 decks shared as PDFs.
  • Series A decks lead with traction, retention, efficient growth and a clear use of funds.

A pitch deck is a short, persuasive slide presentation, and many other business documents are easily confused with it, from business plans and proposals to film treatments and information memoranda. Knowing the differences helps you prepare the right document for each audience. I won first prizes at Changethon 2025 with KrishGyan, Product Genesis at Vividhotsava 2025 with BrandHive and BYTEBATTLE with Annapurna, so this page compares pitch decks with seven related documents and covers orientation, Series A decks and design.

Is a pitch deck the same as a business plan?

No. A pitch deck is a short visual presentation, usually 10 to 15 slides, designed to spark interest and win a meeting or investment. A business plan is a detailed written document covering strategy, operations, market analysis and financial projections.

Also asked as: is pitch deck the same as business plan

Investors today usually ask for a deck first and request deeper materials, such as financial models, during due diligence. Business plans remain useful for bank loans, grant applications, some competitions and internal planning, forcing founders to think through details. Many teams write a concise business plan or memo to clarify thinking, then distil it into a deck. The deck tells the story; the plan provides the evidence behind it.

What is the difference between a pitch deck and a proposal?

A proposal is a document offering to do specific work for a client or funder, detailing scope, approach, deliverables, timeline and budget, often in response to a request. A pitch deck persuades investors or judges to back a venture.

Also asked as: difference between pitch deck and proposal

Proposals are judged on fit with stated requirements and feasibility, and often follow a required format, such as grant applications or tenders. Pitch decks have more freedom and focus on opportunity, traction and team. Some situations blend them, such as pitching a pilot to a company, where slides open the conversation and a proposal formalises the agreement. Match the document to what the audience must decide.

What is the difference between a pitch deck and a portfolio?

A portfolio showcases past work, such as designs, projects or case studies, to demonstrate skill and credibility. A pitch deck argues for a future venture or project, asking the audience to invest, partner or award a prize.

Also asked as: difference between pitch deck and portfolio

Portfolios support hiring or client decisions by showing what you have done; decks support investment decisions by showing what you will do and why it will succeed. A pitch deck may include a slide on past achievements, such as hackathon wins or shipped products, which works like a mini portfolio for the team. Keep portfolios visual and evidence-rich, and decks focused on a single persuasive story. Link to your portfolio from the deck for curious readers.

What is the difference between a pitch deck and a treatment?

A treatment is a prose summary of a film or television story, describing plot, characters and tone, used to pitch creative projects to producers. A pitch deck presents a business opportunity to investors with market, traction and financial information.

Also asked as: difference between pitch deck and treatment

Film and TV pitches sometimes use decks too, called lookbooks or pitch decks, combining visuals, comparable titles and audience information with the treatment's story. Startups in media may need both: a treatment for the creative project and a business deck for the company producing it. Treatments emphasise storytelling quality, while business decks emphasise evidence of demand and a credible plan to make money. Know your audience's industry norms before preparing either.

What is the difference between a pitch deck and a project report?

A project report documents completed or ongoing work in detail, such as objectives, methods, results and conclusions, often for academic evaluation or stakeholders. A pitch deck persuades an audience to support what comes next.

Also asked as: difference between pitch deck and project report

Students often turn final year project reports into pitch decks for hackathons or incubators, which requires cutting detail and reframing: problem, solution, proof, impact and ask. Reports should be thorough and accurate; decks should be concise and memorable. Keep the report as supporting evidence, sharing it when judges or investors ask for depth, and use appendices for technical details they may request. Mixing the two produces cluttered slides that lose the audience quickly.

What is the difference between a pitch deck and a company profile?

A company profile introduces an established business to customers, partners or suppliers, covering history, services, clients and credentials. A pitch deck targets investors or judges with a specific ask, such as funding, and argues for future growth.

Also asked as: difference between pitch deck and company profile

Profiles are descriptive and credibility-focused, often used in sales and procurement, while decks are persuasive and opportunity-focused. A startup may maintain both: a profile for business development and a deck for fundraising. Avoid sending a company profile to investors, since it rarely answers their questions about market size, traction, unit economics and the use of funds. Tailor each document to the reader's decision.

What is the difference between a pitch deck and an information memorandum?

An information memorandum is a detailed, confidential document prepared for selling a company or raising significant capital, often by advisers, covering operations, financials, risks and market in depth. A pitch deck is a short introduction to spark interest.

Also asked as: difference between pitch deck and information memorandum

Investors typically see a teaser or deck first, sign a confidentiality agreement, then receive the information memorandum for due diligence. Memoranda can run to dozens of pages with audited financials and legal details. Early-stage startups rarely need one, while later-stage fundraising and acquisitions often do. Accuracy matters greatly, since memoranda inform major financial decisions and may carry legal responsibilities.

Should a pitch deck be landscape or portrait?

Landscape, almost always. Most decks use a 16:9 widescreen format because they are presented on screens and projectors and read on laptops. Portrait suits printed documents or mobile-first reading but looks awkward when presented.

Send decks as PDFs so layouts stay consistent across devices. If many readers view your deck on phones, keep text large and slides simple so they remain readable when shrunk. Vertical formats can work for social media summaries of your pitch, but keep the main deck landscape. Test the deck on the actual projector or video call setup you will use, since aspect ratios sometimes differ.

How do you build a great Series A pitch and deck?

A Series A deck must prove the business works and can scale: strong traction such as revenue growth, retention and efficient customer acquisition, a large market, a repeatable go-to-market approach, a capable team, and a clear plan for using funds.

Also asked as: how to build a great series a pitch and deck

Lead with your strongest metrics and show trends over time, not just snapshots. Explain what you have learned since earlier funding and why now is the moment to scale. Present milestones the round will achieve, such as market expansion or product launches. Prepare a data room with financials, contracts and metrics definitions for due diligence. Warm introductions from trusted contacts improve response rates. Practise answering hard questions on churn, competition and margins confidently.

How do you design a better pitch deck?

Use one idea per slide, large readable fonts, consistent colours and typography, strong visuals such as product screenshots and simple charts, plenty of whitespace and a clear narrative from problem to ask.

Also asked as: how to design a better pitch deck (2015

Label charts clearly and highlight the key takeaway in each slide's title, so readers grasp the point in seconds. Replace bullet-heavy slides with diagrams or a single bold statement. Use real product images and customer evidence instead of stock photos. Keep design consistent with your brand, and check contrast for readability. Templates help, but customise them to your story. Test with someone unfamiliar with your project; if they cannot explain your idea after viewing it, simplify.
