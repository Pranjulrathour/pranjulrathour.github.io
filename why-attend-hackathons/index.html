Why attend hackathons? How to participate, how long they last, AI rules, and platforms

Key takeaways

  • Attend hackathons to learn fast, build portfolio projects, meet mentors and recruiters, and win prizes.
  • Most last 24 to 48 hours; online hackathons can run for days or weeks.
  • AI tools are usually allowed if rules permit and you disclose use; judges value your own understanding.
  • Common criticisms, such as burnout and shallow projects, are avoidable with good choices and pacing.
  • Find events on Devfolio, Unstop, Devpost, Major League Hacking, HackerEarth and Agorize.

Attend hackathons to learn faster than any course allows, build portfolio projects under real constraints, meet mentors, teammates and recruiters, and sometimes win prizes or opportunities. Even without winning, you leave with new skills and a demo. I have won first prizes at Changethon 2025 at IIT Roorkee, Product Genesis at Vividhotsava 2025 and BYTEBATTLE by MeetKats, and mentored at more than ten hackathons, so this page covers why to attend, how to participate, what they are like, duration, AI hackathons and rules, honest criticisms, and where to find events.

Why should I attend hackathons?

Hackathons compress months of learning into a weekend: you pick a problem, learn whatever tools you need, build, debug under pressure and present. You also meet people who share your interests, get feedback from experienced mentors, and gain projects to discuss in interviews.

Also asked as: why attend hackathons

Other benefits: exposure to sponsor technologies and recruiters, confidence presenting to judges, teamwork experience, and sometimes internships, funding or incubation offers for strong teams. Hackathons also reveal what you enjoy, whether front end, AI, design or pitching, which helps choose a career direction. For students, they are among the best investments of a weekend. My own first prizes came from projects that started as hackathon ideas, and the experience of building under pressure shaped how I ship products today.

How do I participate in hackathons?

Find events on platforms such as Devfolio, Unstop, Devpost and Major League Hacking or your college fest pages, register before deadlines, form a team or join one through the event's matching channels, prepare your tools, then build and submit by the deadline.

Also asked as: how to participate in hackathons

Steps:

  • Choose an event matching your interests and level.
  • Register and read rules on team size, eligibility and AI use.
  • Form a balanced team: front end, back end, design, domain knowledge.
  • Prepare a starter template and accounts for APIs and hosting.
  • At the event, pick a narrow problem and build one complete flow.
  • Submit code, a demo video and slides on time.

What are hackathons like?

Hackathons are intense, energetic and social: an opening ceremony with themes and rules, hours of building with teammates, mentor visits, workshops, food and snacks, late-night debugging, and final presentations where judges ask questions.

Also asked as: why i don’t like hackathons

Offline events feel like a mix of a workplace and a festival, with teams spread across halls, music, games and sponsor booths. Online events happen through chat channels, video calls and submission portals. Expect moments of panic when something breaks, and satisfaction when the demo finally works. Most participants remember the people and the learning more than the results.

How long do hackathons last?

Most in-person hackathons last 24 to 48 hours, often over a weekend, with 36 hours common. Shorter formats of 6 to 12 hours exist for beginners or college events, while online hackathons may run for several days or weeks.

Multi-stage events, such as Smart India Hackathon, include idea submission and shortlisting rounds over weeks, followed by a final hackathon lasting 24 to 36 hours. Check each event's schedule: some include workshops and mentoring sessions before building starts. Plan travel and rest accordingly, especially for overnight events.

Why are hackathons so long?

Hackathons last a day or two because building a working prototype, from idea to demo, takes real time: understanding the problem, designing, coding, integrating, testing and preparing a pitch. The length also creates an immersive, focused environment.

The continuous format encourages teams to push through problems, collaborate intensely and learn quickly. However, long events can lead to exhaustion, so good participants pace themselves: plan sleep in shifts, take breaks and eat properly. Many organisers now encourage rest and offer quiet spaces. Shorter formats exist for those who prefer not to stay overnight.

What are AI hackathons?

AI hackathons are events focused on building with artificial intelligence: language models, computer vision, speech, agents, RAG systems and machine learning, often sponsored by AI companies providing API credits, models and mentors.

Typical projects include chatbots grounded in documents, AI tools for education, healthcare or agriculture, voice assistants in local languages, and agents automating workflows. Judges look for meaningful use of AI, not just a chatbot wrapper: real data, evaluation, safety considerations and a clear problem. KrishGyan, which gives farmers advice in their own voice and language, is the kind of AI project that stands out.

Are AI tools allowed in hackathons?

Usually yes, but rules vary. Many hackathons allow AI coding assistants and AI APIs, sometimes requiring disclosure; some restrict pre-built code or certain tools to keep competition fair. Always read the rules and ask organisers if unsure.

Also asked as: are ai allowed in hackathons

Even where allowed, judges evaluate your understanding: you should explain the architecture, choices and code. Using AI to write boilerplate is common; submitting a project you cannot explain is risky. If your project uses AI as a feature, mention which models and services you used, and how you handled accuracy, privacy and costs.

How can I use AI during hackathons effectively?

Use AI coding assistants to scaffold projects, generate boilerplate, debug errors and write documentation quickly, and use AI APIs to add capabilities such as summarisation, search or vision that would otherwise take weeks to build.

Also asked as: can you use ai during hackathons

Tips:

  • Prepare prompts and starter code before the event, if rules allow.
  • Use AI for repetitive code; focus your time on the core idea.
  • Test AI-generated code immediately; integration bugs are common.
  • Keep API keys secure and monitor usage limits.
  • Have a fallback if an AI API slows down during the demo.

Rehearse the demo with real AI responses so there are no surprises.

Why do some people not like hackathons?

Common criticisms include exhaustion from overnight coding, projects that are shallow and abandoned afterwards, judging that favours polished pitches over technical depth, exclusion of people with caregiving responsibilities, and companies using hackathons for cheap ideas.

These concerns are valid, and good events address them with reasonable hours, beginner tracks, clear judging criteria and fair intellectual property terms. As a participant, choose well-organised events, rest properly, and continue promising projects afterwards. Hackathons are not for everyone; open source, personal projects and internships offer similar learning without the intensity.

What sites list online hackathons and startup challenges, like Agorize?

Platforms listing hackathons and innovation challenges include Agorize, Devpost, Devfolio, Unstop, HackerEarth, Major League Hacking, Kaggle for data competitions, and college or company event pages. Some focus on student events; others on corporate open innovation challenges.

Also asked as: what are sites like agorize.com,listing startup challenges , online hackathons

Agorize and similar platforms host corporate and startup challenges where students and professionals solve business problems, sometimes with prizes, internships or pilot contracts. Devpost lists many online hackathons globally, while Devfolio and Unstop cover many Indian events. Follow these platforms and set alerts for themes you care about. Check each listing's organiser, rules and prize terms, and prefer events with mentors and transparent judging.
