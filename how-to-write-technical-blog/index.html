What is a technical blog? How to start, write great posts, length, and examples

What a technical blog and post are, how to start one, writing great posts, ideal length, examples, the best platforms, and blogs in technical writing.

Key takeaways

  • A technical blog shares explanations, tutorials and lessons about technology, written for practitioners.
  • Start with what you just learned or built; write for the reader one step behind you.
  • Great posts solve one specific problem with clear steps, working code, visuals and honest trade-offs.
  • Length should fit the problem: often 1,000 to 2,500 words for tutorials, shorter for quick fixes.
  • Publish on your own domain or a static site, and cross-post to developer platforms for reach.

A technical blog is a blog where developers, engineers or companies share explanations, tutorials, project write-ups and lessons about technology, written for practitioners who want to understand or build something. Writing one deepens your own understanding, builds a public record of your skills, and helps others solve problems. I document projects such as RAG.NextUpgrad and FineTune Studio in public write-ups, so this page covers what technical blogs and posts are, starting and creating one, writing great posts, length, examples, platforms, and blogs in technical communication.

What is a technical blog?

A technical blog publishes articles about technical topics: how-to tutorials, explanations of concepts, project case studies, debugging stories, comparisons of tools, and opinion pieces grounded in experience. Its audience is usually developers, engineers, students or technical decision-makers.

Also asked as: what is technical blog

Individuals use technical blogs to learn in public and build reputation; companies use engineering blogs to share how they build systems, attract talent and support users. The best technical blogs combine accuracy with clear writing and practical value: readers should leave able to do or understand something they could not before. Many developers say writing regularly made them better engineers, because explaining a system exposes gaps that coding alone can hide.

What is a technical blog post?

A technical blog post is a single article that explains a specific technical topic or solves a specific problem, often with code, diagrams, commands and results. Common types are tutorials, explainers, case studies, troubleshooting guides and comparisons.

A strong post has a clear title stating the problem, a short introduction with context and what the reader will get, structured sections with steps or explanations, working code samples, visuals, a summary, and links to sources or repositories. It respects the reader's time by getting to the point and showing rather than only telling. It also states the versions of tools and libraries used, so readers know whether the instructions still apply.

How do I start a technical blog?

Pick a platform, choose a focus area, and publish your first post about something you recently learned or built. Do not wait for the perfect design or a long list of ideas; consistency matters more than launch polish.

Also asked as: how to start technical blog

Starting steps:

  • Choose topics you work on, such as RAG, web development or DevOps.
  • Set up a blog on your own domain or a developer platform.
  • Write your first post on a problem you solved this month.
  • Publish on a regular schedule, such as twice a month.
  • Share posts where your audience is, such as LinkedIn and communities.
  • Keep a list of ideas from questions you get asked.

How do I create a technical blog site?

Create one with a static site generator, such as Astro, Hugo, Jekyll or Next.js, hosted free on GitHub Pages, Vercel or Netlify, or use a developer blogging platform such as Hashnode or dev.to that handles hosting for you.

Also asked as: how to create technical blog

Owning your domain is valuable long term: your content and search ranking stay yours even if platforms change. A common approach is to publish on your own site and cross-post to dev.to or Hashnode with a canonical link pointing back. Make sure the site supports code highlighting, images, RSS, good mobile reading and fast loading. Keep posts in Markdown in a git repository for easy versioning. Add simple analytics to learn which posts readers find through search, and write more on those topics.

How do I write a technical blog post?

Start with the reader's problem, show the solution step by step with working code and results, explain why each step matters, and end with a summary and next steps. Write a draft quickly, then edit for clarity and accuracy.

Also asked as: how to write a technical blog post

A writing process:

  • Define the reader and the one thing they should learn.
  • Outline sections before writing.
  • Write the steps and code first, then the explanations.
  • Test every code sample exactly as published.
  • Add diagrams or screenshots where words are slow.
  • Cut unnecessary words and jargon; define terms you keep.
  • Ask someone to follow the post and note where they got stuck.

What makes a great technical blog?

Great technical blogs are accurate, specific, practical and honest. Each post solves a real problem, includes tested code and evidence, explains trade-offs, and admits limitations. Readers trust them because the author clearly built and measured what they describe.

Other qualities: clear titles that match what people search for; consistent publishing; readable formatting with headings, short paragraphs and code highlighting; diagrams for architecture; and links to sources and repositories. Original experience is the strongest differentiator, such as "how we cut retrieval failures by adding keyword search", which generic AI-written content cannot copy.

How long should a technical blog post be?

As long as needed to solve the problem completely, and no longer. Quick fixes can be a few hundred words; tutorials and deep dives often run 1,000 to 2,500 words or more. Readers value completeness more than brevity for technical topics.

Structure matters more than length: use headings so readers can skim to the part they need, put the answer or key steps early, and move long background to linked posts. If a post grows beyond 3,000 words, consider splitting it into a series. Measure engagement, such as time on page and comments, to learn what your readers find useful.

What are some technical blog examples?

Strong examples include company engineering blogs from Netflix, Stripe, Cloudflare, Uber and Airbnb, which explain how they build large systems, and individual blogs by engineers who write deep, practical posts on programming, AI and infrastructure.

Study examples for structure and tone: how they state the problem, use diagrams, share numbers, and explain decisions. Cloudflare's posts are known for detailed incident analyses; Stripe's for clarity; many individual blogs for honest learning journeys. For AI topics, look for posts that include evaluation results rather than just demos, which is what separates practical engineering writing from hype.

What are the best technical blog sites and platforms?

For hosting your own blog, GitHub Pages, Vercel and Netlify with a static site generator are popular and free. For built-in audiences, dev.to, Hashnode and Medium are common developer platforms. For reading, engineering blogs and aggregators such as Hacker News surface quality posts.

Choose based on goals: own your domain for long-term reputation and search ranking; use platforms for initial reach; combine both with canonical links. Avoid platforms that paywall or restrict your content if you want maximum reach. Whatever you use, keep source files backed up so you can move platforms later. Writing consistently in one place builds an audience faster than scattering posts across many platforms.

What is a blog in technical communication?

In technical communication, a blog is an informal, regularly updated channel for sharing technical information, such as product updates, tutorials, release notes, best practices and stories, complementing formal documentation such as manuals and API references.

Also asked as: what is blog in technical communication

Blogs are more conversational and timely than documentation, helping explain why changes happened and how to use new features. Technical writers use them to reach users, gather feedback and support community learning. Frameworks such as Diátaxis distinguish tutorials, how-to guides, reference and explanation; blog posts often blend tutorial and explanation styles, linking to official documentation for reference details.
