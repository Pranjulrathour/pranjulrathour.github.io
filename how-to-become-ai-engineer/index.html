What course should I do to become an AI engineer? Paths after BCA, MCA or no degree

Courses and paths to become an AI engineer, with or without a degree, after BCA or MCA, ML vs AI engineers, projects, demand, and whether AI will replace them.

Key takeaways

  • Any computing degree, such as B.Tech, BCA, B.Sc or MCA, works; skills and shipped projects decide AI engineering jobs.
  • Learn Python, software engineering, LLM APIs, RAG, evaluation, agents and deployment, in that order.
  • ML engineers focus on training and serving models; AI engineers focus on building products with existing models.
  • The best portfolio projects solve real problems, are deployed, and show evaluation results.
  • AI engineers are in demand; AI changes how they work but raises the value of engineering judgement.

To become an AI engineer, a computing degree such as B.Tech, BCA, B.Sc or MCA helps, but the course that matters most is the one you build yourself: Python and software engineering, then language model APIs, retrieval-augmented generation, evaluation, agents and deployment, proven through real projects. I am a BCA student who leads engineering at SCULT INDIA and shipped five production AI apps in 2026, so this page covers courses, paths with BCA, MCA or no degree, ML versus AI engineers, LLM engineering, projects, demand and AI's effect on the role.

What course should I do to become an AI engineer?

Choose a computing degree you can access, such as B.Tech in computer science or AI, BCA, B.Sc computer science or MCA, then add focused learning through free and paid courses aligned with AI engineering. The degree opens doors; skills and projects get you hired.

Also asked as: what course should i do to become ai engineer

Useful courses: Harvard's free CS50 and CS50 AI for fundamentals; DeepLearning.AI short courses on LLM apps, RAG and agents; Hugging Face courses on transformers; and cloud provider courses on deployment. Follow a roadmap such as roadmap.sh's AI Engineer map. Do not collect certificates without building: after each course, ship a project that uses what you learned. Pair formal study with one long-running project you keep improving, because depth in one real system teaches retrieval, evaluation, deployment and debugging together, which no single course covers.

How do I become an AI engineer?

Build strong programming and software engineering skills, learn to use language models through APIs, master retrieval and evaluation, then agents and deployment, while shipping projects and joining hackathons. Internships and open-source contributions convert skills into job offers.

Also asked as: how to become an a.i engineer

A practical sequence:

  • Python, Git, APIs, SQL and one web framework.
  • LLM fundamentals: prompting, structured output, costs.
  • RAG: embeddings, chunking, hybrid search, reranking, citations.
  • Evaluation: test sets and metrics.
  • Agents and tools, including MCP.
  • Deployment: FastAPI, Docker, cloud, monitoring.

Document every project with a write-up and demo, and share it publicly. Hackathons accelerate this: building under time pressure with feedback from judges and mentors teaches prioritisation that self-study rarely does.

How do I become a machine learning engineer without a degree?

It is possible but harder, because some employers filter by degree. Compensate with a strong public portfolio, open-source contributions, competition results, certifications from recognised providers, and networking that leads to referrals.

Also asked as: how to become machine learning engineer without a degree

Learn mathematics fundamentals, linear algebra, probability and statistics, alongside Python, machine learning with scikit-learn, deep learning with PyTorch and deployment. Build projects that show the full lifecycle: data collection, training, evaluation, serving and monitoring. Startups and smaller companies are often more open to candidates without degrees. Consider distance or online degrees from recognised universities if formal credentials become a barrier.

Can I become an AI engineer after BCA?

Yes. BCA teaches programming, databases and web development, a solid base for AI engineering, which is mostly about building software around models. Many AI engineering roles care about what you can build, not whether your degree says B.Tech.

Also asked as: can i become ai engineer after bca

My path is an example: during BCA, I built and shipped production AI apps, including a RAG platform, a fine-tuning studio and browser-based face recognition, and won national hackathons. BCA students should start early: learn AI tools in the second year, join hackathons, seek internships, and build a portfolio. Some employers prefer B.Tech or postgraduates for certain roles, which MCA can address later if needed.

Can I become an AI engineer after MCA?

Yes. MCA adds depth in software engineering and computing, and its two-year programme gives time for AI specialisation, a major project and an internship. MCA graduates regularly move into AI and ML roles.

Also asked as: can i become ai engineer after mca

Use MCA strategically: choose AI-related electives, make your final project an AI system with real users and evaluation, do an AI-focused internship, and build a portfolio alongside coursework. Learn practical tools the syllabus may not cover, such as LLM APIs, vector search and deployment. Employers will judge your projects and problem-solving more than the degree title.

What is the difference between an ML engineer and an AI engineer?

ML engineers typically train, optimise and deploy machine learning models, working with data pipelines, feature engineering, training infrastructure and model serving. AI engineers typically build applications using existing models, especially language models, through APIs, retrieval, agents and product integration.

Also asked as: ml vs ai engineer

The roles overlap and titles vary by company. ML engineering demands more mathematics and modelling depth; AI engineering demands more software and product skills, plus evaluation of model behaviour. Both need strong coding and deployment skills. Many engineers move between them; fine-tuning work, like QLoRA in FineTune Studio, sits right at the boundary.

How do I become a great LLM engineer?

Go beyond calling APIs: master retrieval quality, evaluation, prompt and context design, fine-tuning when appropriate, inference optimisation, cost control and safety. Great LLM engineers measure everything and know why their systems fail.

Also asked as: how to become a great llm engineer

Habits of strong LLM engineers:

  • Build evaluation sets before optimising.
  • Read failures by hand, not just metrics.
  • Understand tokenisation, context limits and sampling.
  • Know when to use RAG, fine-tuning or both.
  • Design for failure with fallbacks and refusals.
  • Keep up with research but test claims on your own data.

Ship systems people use, and write about what you learned. Explaining a failure you fixed, such as retrieval missing exact product codes until you added keyword search, shows more skill than listing frameworks.

What is the best AI engineer project for a portfolio?

The best project solves a real problem for real users, is deployed with a public demo, includes evaluation results, and has a clear write-up explaining architecture and trade-offs. Depth beats variety: one excellent project outweighs ten tutorials.

Strong examples: a RAG assistant over real documents with hybrid search, citations and a refusal threshold; a document extraction tool with schema validation; a fine-tuned small model with base-versus-tuned comparisons; a browser-based vision app that protects privacy; or an agent that automates a real workflow with logs and approvals. My own portfolio grew from projects like these, each with a live demo.

Are AI engineers in demand?

Yes. Organisations across industries are building AI features, assistants and automations, and need engineers who can integrate models reliably, securely and cost-effectively. Reports such as the World Economic Forum's Future of Jobs place AI and big data skills among the fastest-growing.

Also asked as: are ai engineers going to be replaced by ai

Demand is strongest for engineers who can ship production systems, not just prototypes: retrieval, evaluation, deployment, monitoring and security. Entry-level competition is high, so portfolios, internships and hackathon results matter. Demand also extends beyond pure AI companies: banks, healthcare, retail, education and government teams all need people who understand both software and AI.

Will AI replace AI engineers?

AI is automating parts of the job, such as writing boilerplate code, generating tests and drafting prompts, but it increases demand for engineers who can design systems, judge quality, ensure safety and take responsibility for outcomes.

The role is shifting from writing every line to specifying, supervising and verifying AI-generated work, and from building single features to orchestrating agents and pipelines. Engineers who adapt, using AI tools heavily while deepening judgement in architecture, evaluation and security, become more productive and more valuable. Those who only do routine coding face more pressure, so focus on skills that require understanding, not repetition.
